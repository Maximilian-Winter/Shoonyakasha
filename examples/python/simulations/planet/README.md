# Planet

A planet of radius 100 km with procedural terrain (continents, oceans, ridged mountains, hills) on a cube-sphere with quadtree level of detail, an ocean and an atmosphere, flown around from a few metres above its ground to 5,000 km away. Its chunks are built on the GPU by a compute pass and drawn with indirect draws. It runs on the planet [pipeline](../../../../python/shoonyakasha/pipelines/planet/pipeline.json) with reverse-Z depth, HDR, an atmosphere pass, a wireframe pass switched on with **F3**, and a canvas overlay. Needs numpy, Pillow and a GPU with 64-bit float shaders (`shaderFloat64`).

The planet, its moon and everything the pipeline draws of them are `PlanetWorld` of [`shoonyakasha.planet`](../../../../docs/api/python/planet.md), with its cube-sphere, quadtree, shadow cascades, orbits and ground textures; the cards, the hint bar, the controls sheet and the settings menu are [`shoonyakasha.toolkit.overlay`](../../../../python/shoonyakasha/toolkit/overlay.py). This example adds the free camera, the player on foot and its gravity gun, the ship and its flight model, the cockpits, the head-up display and the displays, the blocks, the crates, the presets and the checks.

```
python -m shoonyakasha.planet.ground_textures       # download the ground's textures (once)
python -m shoonyakasha.planet.vegetation_textures   # download the leaf and bark scans (once)
python planet.py
python planet.py --capture DIR     # screenshots at the presets, then exit
python planet.py --capture DIR --shots NAME...   # only the shots whose names contain one of these
python planet.py --generated-cockpit   # start with the generated cockpit instead of the model's cabin
python planet.py --verify          # check the GPU's chunks against cube_sphere.chunk_mesh, then exit
python planet.py --orbit-check     # fly one orbit from preset 9 at 16x, report the drift, then exit
python planet.py --landing-check   # land the ship on a mountain near the site, report how it stands, then exit
python planet.py --moon-landing-check   # the same on a crater's floor on the moon
python planet.py --crack-check     # measure the gaps between levels in steep terrain against the skirts, then exit
python planet.py --morph-check     # come down from 20 km, check every swap of chunks happens fully morphed, then exit
python planet.py --prop-rest-check   # drop crates on the planet and the moon, check they come to rest and stay there
python planet.py --walk-check      # walk 200 m circles on the planet and the moon, check the feet stay on the ground
python planet.py --hold-check      # hold a crate with the gravity gun while turning, walking, jumping and rebasing
python planet.py --punt-check      # punt crates and the ground, check the speeds, the recoil and the jump's height
python planet.py --trunk-check     # walk into the trees nearest the blocks, check the feet stop at their trunks
python planet.py --promotion-check   # grab the rocks near the player one by one, check each is drawn once
python planet.py --orbit-crate-check   # a crate in a low orbit of the moon comes round to where it started
python planet.py --save-check      # move rocks, throw a crate, save, build a new planet, load, check what came back
```

## How it works

- **Positions** are Python floats (64-bit), with the planet's centre at the origin. The engine's camera stays at its own origin and only turns. Every frame each chunk and entity is placed at its position minus the camera's, so the GPU only sees numbers that are small near the camera.
- **The projection** is reverse-Z with an infinite far plane (`scene.set_camera_reverse_z`): depth 1 at the 5 cm near plane and 0 at infinity. The passes compare with `greater_or_equal`, and the terrain pass clears depth to 0.
- **The sphere** ([`cube_sphere.py`](../../../../python/shoonyakasha/planet/cube_sphere.py)) has six cube faces. Each face has a frame (normal, u axis, v axis) with u × v = normal, and a point normal + u·u_axis + v·v_axis, with u and v in [−1, 1], is mapped onto the sphere by `spherify`. A chunk is a square of a face at a quadtree level: 16 × 16 quads, its vertices relative to its own centre.
- **The terrain** ([`terrain.glsl`](../../../../python/shoonyakasha/pipelines/planet/shaders/terrain.glsl)) is a height in metres above the sphere for each direction from the planet's centre: domain-warped fBm continents (below zero is sea floor, down to 1,500 m), ridged mountains in bands where a low-frequency mask allows them (3,000 m), hills (150 m), and detail on land from wavelengths of 80 m down to 10 m (15 m in the mountains' bands, 35 % of that outside them), all from 3D gradient noise that hashes integer lattice points. The hills and the detail are eroded fBm: each octave is divided by 1 + erosion × the squared length of the noise gradients summed over the octaves before it (erosion 0.5), so slopes stay smooth and the small features gather on flats and crests. Its parameters are custom values (`planet.terrain.*`) read by the `TerrainParams` uniform buffer. Around the blocks' site the ground is flat within 150 m, at the site's natural height, easing back into the natural terrain by 600 m.
- **Crates** ([`props.py`](props.py)) are rigid bodies in a physics bubble around the player on foot, or without a player the viewpoint (the camera, or the ship in ship mode): [`shoonyakasha.planet.bubble`](../../../../python/shoonyakasha/planet/bubble.py) on `sk.PlanetPhysics` (Jolt Physics, single precision). The bubble's coordinates are relative to the centre of the body under the viewpoint, minus an origin near it; when the viewpoint is 500 m from the origin, the origin moves to it and every body moves back by as much, so positions in the physics stay small near the viewpoint. The bubble steps in steps of 1/120 s in real time, whatever the time warp, at most 8 a frame. Before each step every awake crate is pulled towards the body's centre with GM / r²; on the moon the frame moves with the moon, so its gravity is the moon's alone. The ground is a mesh of 65 × 65 terrain heights 0.5 m apart (32 m across) around the viewpoint ([`collider_probes.py`](../../../../python/shoonyakasha/planet/collider_probes.py)), measured by the `ColliderProbes` pass ([`collider_probes.comp`](../../../../python/shoonyakasha/pipelines/planet/shaders/collider_probes.comp)) with the terrain function the chunks are built from, on the planet with the sea as a floor at sea level. Its grid lies in a plane tangent to the body at a fixed direction near the bubble, so a new mesh over the same ground has the same points; a new one is asked for when the viewpoint, ahead along its velocity by the probes' latency, is more than 8 m east, west, north or south of the mesh's centre, and the old one stays until its heights come back. A second, coarse mesh of 65 × 65 heights 4 m apart (256 m across) is kept around the viewpoint the same way, sharing the probe pass (one patch measured a frame). Bodies collide with one or the other (`set_far`): a prop over the fine mesh with the fine one, else with the coarse one, so a crate rolling or thrown beyond 16 m still lands on ground. Without a player, the bubble is dropped when the viewpoint goes over the other body or more than 2 km above the ground; its props stay in the world (see far props).
- **Far props** ([`props.py`](props.py)): a prop leaves the physics when it is more than 2 km from the bubble's origin, or over neither mesh and less than 50 m above the ground. Out of the physics it flies in doubles around the body it is reckoned around, by that body's gravity (switching body at the moon's sphere of influence) and on the planet the air's drag (drag coefficient 1 on 60 % of its sphere's cross-section, as inside the physics for props faster than 5 m/s), in steps of 1/120 s of the world's clock, so the time warp speeds it up. In the physics a prop faster than 5 m/s is not damped, so a thrown one keeps its speed as it would in flight, and a slower one is damped as Jolt does by default, which helps it settle. The ground under flying props, and under props in the physics over neither mesh, is measured with the world's four extra probes, the longest unmeasured first; a flying prop whose height above that ground, less its speed downwards times the probes' latency, is below its rest height lands there and rests. A prop slower than 0.5 m/s leaving the physics on its ground rests where it is. A prop out of the physics comes back in when it is within 1.9 km of the bubble's origin on its body and over a mesh, or flying more than 100 m above the ground, moving as it was. When the bubble goes, its props leave the physics where they are. **T** on foot tracks the prop the gun punted last: its path (`flight.orbit_path`, around its body) takes the place of the ship's, and the card shows its state, distance, height and speed. The debug card counts the props in the physics, flying and resting.
- **Saving** (**F5**, **F9**): `planet_save.json` beside `planet.py` holds the world's clock (which places the moon), the planet's terrain parameters (its seed among them), the taken rocks' cells, every prop as a record, the ship (position, velocity, orientation, spin, whether it rests, touches the ground or crashed; its throttle and assist; the body it is parked on and where), the mode, the free camera's position and direction, and the player's feet and heading on its body. A prop's record is its kind (a crate or a rock's kind), the body it is reckoned around, its state, its position from that body's centre, rotation, velocity and spin, its size, radius, rest height, mass, entity scale and colour, a rock's cell and the ground under it as last measured; one in the physics is saved resting when asleep and flying otherwise. Loading drops the bubble and every prop, sets the clock, builds the planet again if its parameters differ, puts the taken cells back (the scatter leaves their rocks out), makes every prop again out of the physics (it returns to the physics as any far prop does: a resting one asleep, over the fine mesh), and puts back the ship, the camera and the player. Whenever the planet is built again (**N**, **M / J**, **H / G**, the menu's **Apply**), the props on it are removed and its taken cells cleared, so the scatter's rocks are back; the moon's stay.
- **On foot** ([`player.py`](player.py)): the player is a capsule 1.8 m tall and 0.3 m in radius in the bubble (`sk.PlanetPhysics`' characters, Jolt's `CharacterVirtual`), its up the way away from the body's centre at its feet, set every step, so it stands upright anywhere on the planet or the moon. Its view is a heading along the ground, made square to the up again every step, and a pitch of up to 89°. Each physics step the velocity along the ground, relative to what it stands on, is brought towards the wanted one at up to 40 m/s² on the ground and 6 m/s² in the air. On the ground the velocity along the up is one step of gravity downwards, so it stays on the floor over crests, or 3.3 m/s for a jump (it counts as off the ground for 0.2 s after); in the air gravity pulls it down and the jetpack pushes it up at 9 m/s², burning a quarter of its fuel a second, refilled at 15 % a second on the ground. Jolt's update slides it along what it touches, steps it up onto steps up to 0.4 m and keeps it on the floor down to 0.5 m. Where the terrain mesh does not reach (1 m inside its edges) it is held in place. The camera is at its eye, 1.65 m above the feet (1 m crouched), lowered by a sudden step up and brought back over 0.1 s, with a 2.5 cm head bob every 1.6 m walked (the menu's **Head bob**). The mouse is captured on foot and freed in the other modes and while the menu or the controls are open. On foot the time warp (the menu's **Time warp**) runs only while no prop in the physics is awake, else at 1×, and the bubble's physics runs at 1× whatever the warp; the body under the player splits to level 14 instead of 12, so the ground drawn near the player follows the terrain mesh it stands on more closely (`--walk-check` measures how closely).
- **Tree trunks** ([`trunks.py`](trunks.py)): while there is a bubble, the `VegetationScatter` pass also lists the trees, cacti and rocks it places within 40 m of the camera in the `NearbyTrees` buffer, which is read back by stamp, each with its base relative to its body's centre at that frame ([`nearby_trees.py`](../../../../python/shoonyakasha/planet/nearby_trees.py)). Each listed plant on the planet gets a static capsule in the bubble, as thick as its mesh's trunk near the foot (times the plant's width factor) and standing along the up from 0.5 m below its base to where its trunk thins or forks. The pass lists only plants on the chunks drawn that frame, so a trunk stays until it is more than 50 m from the camera; a plant listed again with another base or kind gets a new capsule, and all trunks go when the terrain or the vegetation changes. Bushes, shrubs and grass have none. The debug card counts them.
- **Rocks** ([`rocks.py`](rocks.py), [`rocks.py` of the package](../../../../python/shoonyakasha/planet/rocks.py)): the scatter places small rocks (0.2 to 0.6 m across, on a grid of 2.4 m cells within 150 m) and boulders (about 0.8 to 2.5 m, on the trees' grid where no tree or bush grows, within 700 m) on both bodies. On the planet they lie on dry biomes (desert, steppe, tundra) and on rock (steep or high ground), fewer on snow, none on beaches or the sea floor; on the moon everywhere, more on slopes, so crater walls and rims hold the most. Each is a lumpy, flat-shaded stone drawn by the vegetation passes, its up away from the centre of the body it stands on, lit on the moon by the sunlight alone. Each rock listed within 40 m gets a static convex hull of its mesh's points from its base up (2,600 kg/m³), kept as the trunks are, and strikes on it are watched. A rock comes loose when the gravity gun grabs, pulls or punts it, or when a dynamic body strikes it with at least 300 kg m/s: its body turns dynamic where it is, its cell goes into the world's taken rocks (a hash table on the GPU, `TakenRocks`, which the scatter looks each rock up in), so the scatter leaves it out from that frame on, and it becomes a prop drawn by a mesh entity of its kind, in its colour. A loose rock removed (beyond the bubble's reach, or with the bubble) gives its cell back, so the rock is back where it lay. The debug card counts the fixed and loose rocks.
- **The gravity gun** ([`gun.py`](gun.py)) acts once per physics step, after the player. It targets what a ray from the eye hits within 18 m, passing through the player (`cast_ray(..., skip_character=...)`), or else the crate or rock closest to the view's direction within 6° of it that the eye can see. Holding grab grabs one within 4 m and pulls one farther away towards the eye at up to 12 m/s (at most 6 kN), keeping it as the target while it stays within 30° of the view, until it is close enough to grab. One whose weight is more than 90 % of the gun's 9 kN is too heavy: it lifts up to about 1,650 kg on the planet and 8,250 kg on the moon. A held body passes through the player (`set_pass_through`). It is pulled towards the hold point, 2.2 m (1.5 to 6 m with the wheel) plus its radius in front of the eye, as a critically damped 6 Hz spring would, its weight cancelled, at most 9 kN in all; its approach is held to a speed it can stop from with the force left over its weight, so a heavy boulder does not swing past. It is turned towards the rotation it had relative to the view when grabbed (**R** + mouse turns it), and dropped once it has stayed more than 1.2 m from the hold point for 0.25 s. A punt gives the held body, or a target within 6 m, its mass times min(30 m/s, 1,600 N s / its mass) along the view; something that cannot move takes the full 1,600 N s. The player gets 60 % of the impulse back, at most 8 m/s, so punting the ground under the player is a jump of about 6.5 m on the planet and 33 m on the moon. While holding, pressing punt charges instead: let go within 0.25 s for a plain punt, or later for a charged one, at least 240 m/s times the charge (the time held over 1.5 s, at most 1) times min(1, 30 kg / its mass), at most once every 2 s. On the moon a full charge throws a light crate past its escape speed (222 m/s); its circular speed near the ground is about 157 m/s. The gun ([`viewmodel.py`](viewmodel.py)) is built from boxes in the camera's space at the lower right of the view. It lags fast turns, follows the head bob, and kicks back 6 cm and up 3° on a punt. Its tip glows by its state (ready, something it can grab under the reticle, too heavy in red, holding), and while it holds something a wobbling beam runs to it, both drawn unlit by the `Screens` pass.
  - Getting out (**F**) puts the player 1 m above the boarding point, 3.5 m left of the ship's centre line beside the cockpit, level with its gear's feet, and parks the ship: until boarded it moves with the body under it. Getting in (**F**) takes the player within 3 m of that point aboard. While the ship is within 2 km of the bubble's origin it has a kinematic body there, the convex hull of its gear and hull points, moved each physics step along its path that frame, which the player and crates stand on and bump into.
- **Bodies** (`Body` in `shoonyakasha.planet.world`): a body drawn with terrain has an index, a radius, terrain parameters, an orbit giving its centre and velocity at the flight's time (none for the planet, whose centre is the origin), whether the ocean is drawn on it, and its own quadtree. The bodies share the chunk pool. Each body's tree selects its chunks with the camera taken from the body's centre, and a chunk is placed at the body's centre plus its own, less the camera, subtracted in double. Horizon culling is against the chunk's own body. The body under a point is the one it is reckoned around, as `flight.reference` reckons orbits: the moon within its sphere of influence, else the planet. The heights shown above the ground and the sphere, the free camera's level horizon, the probe under the camera and the ground patch follow the body under the camera or the ship, and the free camera moves with the body under it. The bodies are the planet (body 0) and the moon (body 1); the moon's tree is made with the planet's, once the first probes have come back.
- **The moon's terrain** ([`moon.glsl`](../../../../python/shoonyakasha/pipelines/planet/shaders/moon.glsl)), body 1's in `BuildChunks`: craters of seven sizes on rolling ground (fBm, 400 m) with 1.5 m of relief at 20 m wavelength. For each size the scaled direction lies in a lattice of cells; a cell within half a cell of the scaled sphere holds a crater with a chance of 0.6, at a hashed point moved onto the sphere, with a hashed radius of 0.08 to 0.28 cells. The largest cells are a sixth of the radius across (craters up to 1.2 km in radius), each size 2.2 times finer (the smallest up to 10 m). A crater is a bowl with a flat floor and a raised rim, joined by smooth minima and maxima, 0.4 of its radius deep at most; overlapping craters add. A chunk's normals leave out the craters whose radius is under two of its vertex spacings, and the relief under that; its heights keep them all. It is coloured grey (`terrain.frag`): highlands, and darker maria from a low-frequency noise, varied by a finer one, lighter on steep slopes. Seen from the planet, the atmosphere pass lays the air between the camera and it over it, as over everything in the depth buffer.
- **Skirts:** each chunk's edge vertices have copies 5 % of the chunk's edge length lower, joined to the edge by walls drawn from both sides. They hide the cracks where chunks of different levels meet.
- **Building chunks on the GPU** ([`chunk_build.comp`](../../../../python/shoonyakasha/pipelines/planet/shaders/chunk_build.comp)): `ChunkPool` is a storage buffer of 2048 slots of 357 vertices (the 17 × 17 grid, then 4 × 17 skirt vertices), each four `vec4`s: position and u, normal and v, direction and height, and the sine of the horizon towards the sun (see Shadows). A chunk a body's quadtree wants takes a free slot and a job. Each frame Python sets up to 16 jobs (face, level, x, y, slot, skirt depth, stamp, body) as custom values, and the `BuildChunks` pass builds one job per workgroup of 64 threads; workgroups past the job count return at once.
  - Face coordinates, `spherify`, and the position direction × (radius + height) minus the chunk's centre are worked out in 64-bit floats, with the radius of the job's body (`ChunkJobs.radii`, by its index), and stored as 32-bit. Heights are 32-bit.
  - The grid is built first, then its lowest and highest heights, then the skirts: they reach below their edge by the job's skirt depth (5 % of the chunk's edge) or the grid's height range, whichever is greater. A coarser neighbour's edge is a straight line between vertices of the finer chunk's own grid, so it cannot stand further from the finer edge than that range.
  - A vertex's normal comes from the terrain at four points a short arc away, along two tangents that depend on its direction alone, so a vertex shared by chunks has the same normal in each.
  - Each slot's lowest and highest grid heights go to `ChunkHeights` with the job's stamp, read back every frame. Python gives them to the chunk's quadtree node.
  - A 17th workgroup evaluates the terrain at 176 probes (a direction and a body) into `ProbeHeights`, read back every frame: the ground under the camera (on the body under it; the result is dropped if the camera is over another body by the time it comes back, or more than 1 km from where it was measured), the site's natural height, in ship mode the 169 points of the ground patch around the ship, and in the last four any directions a check asks for. Each result carries the stamp of the frame that asked for it (`ChunkJobs.probeStamp`); results asked for with an older terrain version are ignored. The frames between asking and reading back are the probes' latency.
  - A chunk is drawn in the frame its job runs, since `BuildChunks` comes first. Releasing a chunk frees its slot.
- **Drawing** (`Terrain`): one `draw_indexed_indirect` call with a count. Every chunk shares the 2304 indices of `ChunkIndices`, set once at start. Each frame `sk.PlanetLod` writes `ChunkDraws`, the number of chunks to draw and then one command per chunk with `vertexOffset` at its slot's first vertex and `firstInstance` its slot, and `ChunkInstances`, each slot's centre minus the camera's position (subtracted in double) and its colour. [`terrain.vert`](../../../../python/shoonyakasha/pipelines/planet/shaders/terrain.vert) reads the vertices from `ChunkPool` as its vertex buffer and adds `ChunkInstances[gl_InstanceIndex]`. The blocks are entities, drawn after it by `Blocks`.
- **Climate** ([`climate.glsl`](../../../../python/shoonyakasha/pipelines/planet/shaders/climate.glsl)): `BuildChunks` stores each of the planet's vertices' climate in its fourth `vec4` beside the horizon, and the probes return it with their heights. The temperature is 30 degrees C at the equator falling to -14 at the poles (with the cube of the sine of the latitude, which a noise wavers), plus a noise of about 3 degrees either way, less 8.5 degrees per kilometre above sea level. The moisture is 0.5 + 0.28 cos(6 × latitude) (wet at the equator and near 60 degrees, dry near 30 degrees and at the poles), less up to 0.3 inland, a little more high up, plus a noise about 11 km across. A third value, the cover, is a noise about 400 m across. Vertices morph their climate with their heights.
- **Biomes and colours** ([`biome.glsl`](../../../../python/shoonyakasha/pipelines/planet/shaders/biome.glsl), [`terrain.frag`](../../../../python/shoonyakasha/pipelines/planet/shaders/terrain.frag)): the menu's temperature and moisture offsets are added to the climate, and eight biomes are weighed by it, four temperature bands (below 0, 0 to 7, 7 to 19, above 19 degrees) by four moisture bands (below 0.15, to 0.32, to 0.55, above), each edge blended over 3 degrees or 0.08 of moisture:

  | | dry | | | wet |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | below 0 C | tundra | tundra | tundra | tundra |
  | 0 to 7 C | steppe | steppe | taiga | taiga |
  | 7 to 19 C | desert | steppe | grassland | deciduous forest |
  | above 19 C | desert | desert | savanna | tropical forest |

  The ground is a weighted mix of layers, each with its own colour: sea floor (darker with depth); sand on beaches below 40 m; rock where the slope is steep; snow where the temperature is below -6 degrees, on all but cliffs; and elsewhere each biome's shares of sand, dry ground, grass, forest and rock, coloured by the biome's soil, its plants and its forest canopy, with the forest thinned to grass where the cover is low (clearings). The moon is one layer of its own. The menu's flora and soil hues turn the plants' and the soils' colours around the colour wheel, keeping their brightness; its palettes are named pairs of them. The other colour modes show one colour per cube face or per quadtree level, with lines at every power-of-two subdivision of a face ([`shading.glsl`](../../../../python/shoonyakasha/pipelines/planet/shaders/shading.glsl)), or one flat colour per biome.
- **Ground textures** ([`ground_textures.py`](../../../../python/shoonyakasha/planet/ground_textures.py), [`ground.glsl`](../../../../python/shoonyakasha/pipelines/planet/shaders/ground.glsl)): an albedo and a normal map at 1K for each layer, CC0 scans from Poly Haven (sand, dry ground, grass, rock) and ambientCG (forest floor, snow, the moon), downloaded by `python -m shoonyakasha.planet.ground_textures` into the user's cache (`%LOCALAPPDATA%\shoonyakasha\ground_textures` on Windows; see the [package's page](../../../../docs/api/python/planet.md#ground-textures)) with a `CREDITS.md`. At the start they are read with Pillow and handed to the `Terrain` pass's `groundSet` with `set_pipeline_image_data(..., mipmaps=True)`; without them the ground keeps its colours alone.
  - Each layer with a weight of at least 2 % is sampled by triplanar projection in planet space (each projection's weight the normal's component to the 6th power, normalised), at two scales, 5 m and 47 m a tile. A layer's albedo is its colour times its texture over the texture's mean (from Python, in linear RGB), near scale times far; the moon's takes the texture's brightness alone. The normal maps of the near scale are blended onto the surface's normal (whiteout blend).
  - Texture coordinates are the camera's position relative to the body's centre over the scale, less its whole tiles (in double, in Python, `GroundParams.offsets`), plus the point's position relative to the camera over the scale, so the tiling stays steady at any distance from the centre. The textures are sampled with gradients taken before the branches over layers and projections, through a repeating sampler with 16x anisotropy.
  - Each scale fades out as its tiles shrink on the screen from 48 to 12 pixels across, before their repetition shows; beyond that the ground is its colours alone.
  - The menu's **Ground textures** sets how much they show (0 none), **Ground relief** the normal maps' strength.
- **Vegetation** ([`vegetation.py`](../../../../python/shoonyakasha/planet/vegetation.py), [`vegetation_scatter.comp`](../../../../python/shoonyakasha/pipelines/planet/shaders/vegetation_scatter.comp), [`vegetation.vert`](../../../../python/shoonyakasha/pipelines/planet/shaders/vegetation.vert), [`biome.glsl`](../../../../python/shoonyakasha/pipelines/planet/shaders/biome.glsl)): trees, bushes, shrubs and cacti on the planet within 2.5 km of the camera and grass within 150 m, and the rocks on both bodies, placed on the GPU each frame and drawn with one indirect draw per kind.
  - **Textures** ([`vegetation_textures.py`](../../../../python/shoonyakasha/planet/vegetation_textures.py)): CC0 scans from ambientCG (a leaf set, conifer sprigs, small leafy sprigs, grass blades and bark), downloaded by `python -m shoonyakasha.planet.vegetation_textures` into the user's cache beside the ground's textures, with a `CREDITS.md`. At the start, with Pillow, each leaf, sprig and blade is cut out of its scan (each opaque region of its opacity), turned upright along its longest axis, and composed into the cards of a foliage atlas, eight cards of 256 texels: two twigs of leaves, two conifer branches, a bush, an acacia's sprays, a shrub's bare twigs with a few leaves, and a tuft of grass. Each card's leaves are scaled to a mean of 0.5 (linear) per channel, which the shader multiplies by the plant's colour, over its stems in a dark neutral; transparent texels take the mean, so the mip levels do not darken at the edges. Without the scans, leaves, needles and blades drawn in the module take their place.
  - **Meshes** ([`vegetation.py`](../../../../python/shoonyakasha/planet/vegetation.py)): each plant one unit tall. Trees are tapered tubes of bark (trunks and branches) and alpha-tested cards showing the atlas: a spruce (whorls of drooping conifer branches to the top), a pine (a bare trunk under a rounded crown), a round and a tall broadleaf tree (140 and 110 twig cards through an ellipsoid crown, their bases inside and tips out) and an acacia (a forked trunk under a flat crown). A bush is a mound of cards, a shrub five upright cards of twigs, a cactus a ribbed column with two arms, grass three crossed cards. A card's normal leans three quarters of the way out from its crown's centre, so the crown is lit as a whole, and its shade is darker deep in the crown and low down.
  - **Impostors** ([`vegetation_impostors.py`](../../../../python/shoonyakasha/planet/vegetation_impostors.py)): at the start each tree's mesh is drawn from the side, orthographically, into a 256-texel cell of an impostor atlas (at twice that and averaged down, with a depth buffer and the cards' alpha test), with its normals and which texels are bark. Beyond 250 m a tree is its impostor: one quad as wide as the tree that turns about its up to face the camera (in the shadow cascades, the sun), lit with the impostor's normals.
  - Each cube face is divided into 16,384 x 16,384 cells (about 9.6 m), the same whatever the chunks are; each cell has one candidate at a point jittered by a hash of the cell, which belongs to the drawn chunk (of level 7 or finer) whose face coordinates hold it. `VegetationScatter`, a workgroup per drawn chunk, keeps a candidate within the range by a draw from the hash against its biomes' chances there (from the chunk's climate, `biomePlants`): a tree with a chance of 0.95 in tropical forest, 0.85 in deciduous forest, 0.8 in taiga and a few hundredths in grassland, savanna and steppe, thinned to a fifth in clearings and up to 1.4 times in groves (by the cover); else a bush within 700 m (0.03 to 0.25 by biome); none on beaches, rock, snow or the sea floor. Beyond a distance worked out from the range (`world.vegetation_thinning`: 420 m at the range of 2.5 km) a tree is kept with a chance of the square of that distance over its own, so that where a tree grows in every cell the trees beyond the detailed meshes' distance fill at most 85 % of their kinds' 32,768 instances; the forest's canopy colour on the ground carries it further. A tree is a conifer by its biomes' share of conifers (nearly all in taiga and tundra, half in steppe, a few in deciduous forest and grassland, none in desert, savanna and tropical forest), a spruce or a pine by a draw; else an acacia by their acacias' share (desert, savanna, steppe), else a round or a tall broadleaf tree. A bush is a cactus by their cacti's share (desert), else a shrub by their shrubs' share (tundra, steppe, desert), else a bush. The draws from the hash come in the same order whatever is kept, so a plant keeps its form and size at any distance. A plant stands on the chunk's surface as drawn (morphed), 9 to 18 m tall (spruces), 12 to 22 m (pines), 7 to 13 m and 9 to 16 m (broadleaf), 5 to 9 m (acacias), 1.2 to 2.6 m (bushes), 0.5 to 1.3 m (shrubs) or 2.5 to 7 m (cacti), turned and tinted by the hash and 0.85 to 1.15 times as wide, in its biomes' foliage colour (1.8 times their canopy colour; 60 % of that for conifers, 75 % for the others; a cactus green turned with the flora's hue), and shrinks to nothing over the last 15 % of the range.
  - `Vegetation` draws them after the terrain with the sun's shadow map and the clouds' shadows ([`vegetation.frag`](../../../../python/shoonyakasha/pipelines/planet/shaders/vegetation.frag)): bark from its tile, cards and grass from the atlas times twice the plant's colour, a texel drawn where its alpha is at least 1/2 after it is scaled by 1 + a quarter per mip level (so the averaged levels do not thin the foliage with distance). Bark and cacti are lit as diffuse surfaces. Foliage is lit by the sun wrapped half past its terminator, light through it where the sun is behind it (0.45 of the sunlight on its back), a glow looking towards the sun through it, all reaching it by a quarter in shadow, and 1.6 times the sky light darkened by its shade. Leaf cards sway in the wind by up to 1.5 % of the plant's height. `ShadowVegetation0` to `3` put the trees and bushes into the shadow cascades with the same alpha test (each plant into the cascades whose far split it is within, plus 100 m).
  - Grass ([`grass_scatter.comp`](../../../../python/shoonyakasha/pipelines/planet/shaders/grass_scatter.comp)): `GrassScatter` places tufts the same way within 150 m of the camera, on chunks of level 10 or finer, with its own grid of 131,072 x 131,072 cells per face (about 1.2 m): a tuft by its biomes' grass chance, 0.95 in grassland, 0.9 in steppe, 0.85 in savanna, less in forests and tundra, 0.06 in desert. A tuft is three crossed cards of the atlas's grass, darker at their base, scaled by 0.3 to 0.7 m tall and shrinking to nothing over the last 30 % of the range. `Vegetation` draws it as the last kind in its biomes' grass colour (their plants, towards their soil where the grass is dry: savanna, steppe, desert), its tips sway in the wind (up to 12 % of its height, the phase following its place), its normals lean up so both sides are lit alike, and it casts no shadows.
  - The menu's **Vegetation** switches all of it off, **Vegetation density** scales the chances, **Vegetation range** sets the trees' and bushes' range (500 m to 5 km); **Grass** switches the grass off and **Grass range** sets its range (50 to 400 m).
- **Lighting** ([`atmosphere.glsl`](../../../../python/shoonyakasha/pipelines/planet/shaders/atmosphere.glsl)): the terrain and the blocks are diffuse surfaces lit by the sun, with an illuminance of 20 dimmed by the air between the surface and the sun (from the transmittance table below; none in the planet's shadow), and a bluish sky light that fades as the sun sets. The moon's ground has the sunlight alone (none in the planet's shadow), without sky light or cloud shadows. They are drawn into `hdrColor`, a 16-bit float image. The sun's direction is set by its elevation and azimuth seen from the blocks.
- **Shadows** ([`shadows.py`](../../../../python/shoonyakasha/planet/shadows.py), [`shadow_terrain.vert`](../../../../python/shoonyakasha/pipelines/planet/shaders/shadow_terrain.vert), [`shadow_entity.vert`](../../../../python/shoonyakasha/pipelines/planet/shaders/shadow_entity.vert), [`shadow.glsl`](../../../../python/shoonyakasha/pipelines/planet/shaders/shadow.glsl)): the terrain, the blocks and the ship's model cast the sun's shadow on the terrain, the sea, the blocks and the model, through four cascades that split the view at 12 m, 150 m, 2 km and 20 km (texels of about 1.7 cm, 21 cm, 2.8 m and 28 m).
  - Each frame Python fits each cascade in doubles: an orthographic window along the sun around its slice's bounding sphere (the same size whichever way the camera turns), reaching 5 km further towards the sun so what stands between it and the sun casts into it, snapped to whole texels in planet space so a fixed point keeps its place in its texel as the camera moves. The matrices map positions relative to the camera to the map (`ShadowParams`).
  - `ShadowTerrain0` to `ShadowTerrain3` (one pass repeated, the cascade from `pass.repeatIndex`) draw every selected chunk within 25 km, in view or not, morphed as drawn, depth only into a 2048² layer each of `sunShadowMap` (slope-scaled depth bias, depth clamped). `ShadowEntities0` to `ShadowEntities3` then add the entities on render layers 1 and 2 (the blocks, the ship's model and its displays), unculled, by their world matrices (`ShadowEntityDraw`).
  - A surface picks its cascade by its distance along the view, moves two of the cascade's texels along its normal, and compares with the map at nine places a texel apart, each a filtered comparison. The shadow fades out over the last 2 km; beyond 20 km the map shadows nothing. Off with the menu's **Shadows**, and while the sun is below the camera's horizon.
  - Distant terrain's shadow, at any distance: each chunk vertex holds the sine of the terrain's horizon towards the sun's azimuth, measured when the chunk is built (`chunk_build.comp`): the highest elevation of the terrain (the sea as a floor) at 20 distances along the ground that way, spaced evenly in their logarithm from two of the chunk's vertex spacings (at least 300 m) out to 40 km. It morphs with the vertex. A surface is lit where the sun stands above that horizon, softened over about 1° near the camera and up to about 4° beyond 60 km, where the vertices are kilometres apart. The terrain and the sea take the lesser of this and the map's share; the blocks take the map's.
  - When the sun has moved more than 0.5° from the direction the horizons were measured towards (`ChunkJobs.horizonSun`), every built chunk is built again in its own slot, nearest first, in the jobs left over by new chunks, keeping its old vertices until then; a chunk whose slot already has a job in that frame gets no second one.
  - The sunlight is scaled by the share lit; the sky light is not.
- **The ocean** (`Ocean`, [`ocean.vert`](../../../../python/shoonyakasha/pipelines/planet/shaders/ocean.vert), [`ocean.frag`](../../../../python/shoonyakasha/pipelines/planet/shaders/ocean.frag)): the selected chunks whose height range reaches below 0, drawn again from `ChunkPool`, morphed as the terrain is, with each vertex moved along its direction to sea level (its position minus direction × height), through a second draw list, `OceanDraws`. Where the ground is above the sea it hides the water by the depth test, which draws the coastlines. The water depth comes from the terrain function at each pixel (`terrainSeaFloor`: only the continent noise and the seabed away from the coasts, the whole terrain near them). The surface is blended over the sea floor already drawn (premultiplied alpha):
  - reflected light, by Schlick's Fresnel with water's F0 of 0.02: the sky (the sky light over π) and the sun's highlight (normalised Blinn-Phong, with the sunlight that comes through the air). Its exponent is 400 within 3 km of the camera and falls geometrically to 30 by 20 km, so far water reflects the sun as a wider glint, for the waves too small to draw there;
  - light that enters the water and is scattered back out of it, with the sea floor showing through 1/e as much per 12 m of water the view crosses;
  - ripples 20 m across that move with time and tilt the normal, fading out by 3 km from the camera.
  The ocean writes depth, so the atmosphere is laid over the sea surface, not the sea floor.
- **Temporal anti-aliasing** (`TAA`, [`taa.frag`](../../../../python/shoonyakasha/pipelines/planet/shaders/taa.frag)): the passes that draw into `hdrColor` (terrain, vegetation, blocks, screens, ocean, wireframe) move their positions by a sub-pixel jitter that changes every frame, the Halton (2, 3) sequence's first eight points within half a pixel (`world.taa_jitter`), so the frames sample different points of each pixel. A fullscreen pass then blends each frame into a history: each pixel's point is rebuilt from its depth with the jitter taken out, moved by the camera's motion since the frame before (worked out in double in Python), and projected with the frame before's view and projection (`scene.camera.prevViewProjection`) to where it was then. The history there, sampled with a Catmull-Rom filter so it does not blur as the camera moves, is clamped to the colours of the pixel's 3 × 3 neighbourhood (in YCoCg), and a tenth of this frame is blended in, both as c / (1 + luminance). The history is dropped on the first frame, after turning it on, where it was off the screen, and when the camera jumps more than 5 km in a frame. `TAAHistory` copies the result into the persistent `taaHistory`, and the atmosphere reads the result (`taaColor`) instead of `hdrColor`. The menu's **Anti-aliasing** turns it off. The orbit path and the overlay, drawn after the atmosphere, are not jittered.
- **The atmosphere** (`Atmosphere`, [`atmosphere.frag`](../../../../python/shoonyakasha/pipelines/planet/shaders/atmosphere.frag)): a fullscreen pass that reads `taaColor` (the scene after temporal anti-aliasing) and the depth buffer and writes the swapchain. For each pixel it builds the view ray from the inverse projection and view, takes the distance to the scene from the reverse-Z depth (0 is the sky), and raymarches the light scattered towards the camera from where the ray enters the atmosphere to the scene or its exit: 32 steps, denser near the start. Each step adds sunlight scattered once by air (Rayleigh) and haze (Mie, g = 0.8), with the sun's transmittance from the transmittance table (none in the planet's shadow), and light scattered more than once, from the multiple scattering table. The scene is seen through the transmittance, plus the scattered light; the sky gets a sun disk.
  - Two lookup tables (Hillaire 2020), built every frame by compute passes before the terrain (`TransmittanceLut`, `MultiScatteringLut`, [`transmittance_lut.comp`](../../../../python/shoonyakasha/pipelines/planet/shaders/transmittance_lut.comp), [`multi_scattering_lut.comp`](../../../../python/shoonyakasha/pipelines/planet/shaders/multi_scattering_lut.comp)) into 16-bit float images that `cameraSet` samples linearly:
    - transmittance, 256 × 64: from a height towards the top of the atmosphere along a direction, for directions that clear the planet, in 40 steps;
    - multiple scattering, 32 × 32 by height and the sun's zenith angle: over 64 directions around a point, the sunlight scattered once towards it (by air, haze and ground with an albedo of 0.3) and the share of light the air scatters, summed over all further orders as L₂ / (1 − f<sub>ms</sub>).
  - Ozone absorbs without scattering, in a layer that peaks at 7.8 km and falls to nothing 4.7 km above and below, with Earth's absorption times 3.2 (like the air's). The menu's **Multiple scattering** and **Ozone** switch them off for comparison. The result is scaled by the exposure and tone mapped (`ACESFilm` from `sk/tonemap.glsl`).
  - The atmosphere's top is 20 km up; the air's and the haze's densities fall off with scale heights of 2.5 km and 400 m. Their coefficients are Earth's times 3.2, which keeps Earth's optical depth straight up; **T / Y** scale them. They are custom values (`planet.atmosphere.*`) read by `AtmosphereParams`.
  - Ray and sphere intersections work from the ray's closest point to the planet's centre, which keeps them to about half a metre with the camera 5,000 km out.
  - The camera's position relative to the planet's centre (`planet.cameraFromCentre`, from the doubles) places the rays and the lit surfaces.
- **Clouds** (`Clouds`, [`clouds.frag`](../../../../python/shoonyakasha/pipelines/planet/shaders/clouds.frag), [`clouds.glsl`](../../../../python/shoonyakasha/pipelines/planet/shaders/clouds.glsl)): a layer between 1.5 and 3.5 km up.
  - Density: a coverage noise on the direction from the planet's centre (cells of about 2.5 km), raised so that the menu's **Cloud cover** share of the sky is cloud, less a threshold that rises from nothing at 8 % of the layer's height to all of the coverage at its top, so denser weather reaches higher: each cloud is a dome whose height follows its weather, on a flat base where the density fades in over the layer's lowest 6 %; eroded at the edges by a 3D detail noise (900 m). The noises' octaves are each turned by a fixed rotation, so their lattices do not line up along the axes. The extinction at full density is the menu's **Cloud density** (0.04 per metre).
  - Filtering: an octave of either noise fades out as its lattice cells shrink from 4 to 2 of the cloud pass's pixels across at the point, and the coverage's rise is widened by how much the noise changes across a pixel (at most 0.2), so a cloud's edge is never sharper than a pixel and clouds too small to resolve are left out rather than drawn as single pixels.
  - A fullscreen pass at a quarter of the screen's resolution each way marches each view ray through the layer, up to the scene's depth and at most 50 km of the layer: 64 steps spaced with the square of their index (short near the camera), shifted by a per-pixel, per-frame random amount. At each step the sunlight comes through the atmosphere (the transmittance table) and through the cloud towards the sun (4 steps out to 1.2 km); light scattered many times is approximated by four octaves, each with 0.3 times the extinction, half the anisotropy of the two-lobed Henyey-Greenstein phase function and half the weight of the last; light from all around adds the sky's and 5 % of the sunlight above the clouds (scattered through the whole layer, it lights even their depths), both brighter towards the layer's top. It writes the clouds' light and transmittance (`cloudLayer`) and their mean distance with the scene's distance it marched to (`cloudDistance`).
  - The atmosphere pass takes the 4 x 4 nearest cloud texels, weighted by a cubic B-spline and by how close their scene distance is to the pixel's (a texel 5 % off counts half; so clouds do not bleed over nearer edges), and puts together the air in front of the clouds, the clouds seen through it, and the scene and the air behind them through the clouds.
  - Their shadows: the terrain, the sea and the blocks scale the sunlight by the clouds' transmittance towards the sun, from their density at four points along the sun's direction through the layer (at most 6 km of it), plus 30 % of what the clouds stop, which reaches the ground scattered through them.
  - Wind: each frame the coverage noise moves by the menu's **Wind** speed (15 m/s) times the frame's time and the time warp; the detail noise moves with it, at the same speed in metres.
  - The menu's **Clouds** switches them off.
- **Level of detail** (`sk.PlanetLod`, in C++: [`PlanetLod.h`](../../../../include/Planet/PlanetLod.h); [`quadtree.py`](../../../../python/shoonyakasha/planet/quadtree.py) is the same in Python, which `tests/python/test_planet_native_lod.py` compares it with frame by frame): each face is the root of a quadtree, down to level 12 (chunks 38 m across, quads 2.4 m); on foot the body under the player goes down to level 14 (chunks 9.5 m across, quads 0.6 m), which the split distances keep within about 50 m of the camera. Every frame:
  - A node splits into its four children when the camera is within the split factor (1.25) times its edge length of its bounding sphere. A split node merges again only beyond 1.25 times that.
  - A node's bounding sphere holds its patch of the sphere raised and lowered through its height range, plus 5 % of its edge length. The range is the one its chunk's heights gave, or its parent's until they come back; the roots start with −2,000 to 6,000 m.
  - A node stays drawn until all four of its children are built, so there are no holes.
  - Morphing ([`morph.glsl`](../../../../python/shoonyakasha/pipelines/planet/shaders/morph.glsl), in the terrain's and the ocean's vertex shaders, which read the ancestors' vertices from `ChunkPool`): a chunk's ancestor k levels up has its vertices at every 2<sup>k</sup>-th vertex of the chunk (k up to 4), split along the same diagonals, so its surface at any vertex is a weighted sum of three of the chunk's vertices (`cube_sphere.stride_weights`). Each vertex blends towards its parent's surface, then from there towards its grandparent's, and so on, by smoothstep(0.8 S<sub>k</sub>, S<sub>k</sub>, its distance from the camera), S<sub>k</sub> the k-th ancestor's split distance (the parent's, `ChunkInstances.morph.x`, times 2<sup>k−1</sup>). A parent splits when the camera comes within S<sub>1</sub> of its bounding sphere, which holds the chunk's vertices, so a chunk appears and goes fully blended to its parent's surface; an edge beside a coarser neighbour lies on the neighbour's grid and follows its blending. Normals, directions and heights blend alike; skirts keep their offset from their edge. The ground patch (physics) keeps the true terrain.
  - At most 16 missing chunks are built per frame: those in view first, nearest first. None are built while the pool is full.
  - Chunks no frame has used for 120 frames are released.
  - Of the chunks the tree selects, those whose bounding sphere is out of view or behind the horizon are not drawn (nor redrawn by the ocean or the wireframe). In view: within a cone around the view direction that holds the screen's diagonal. Behind the horizon (`hiddenByHorizon`, as `quadtree.hidden_by_horizon`): wholly inside the cone from the camera tangent to a sphere at the terrain's floor and beyond the circle where that cone touches it. The floor is the deepest the terrain can go: 10 % more than the ocean depth, or than 5 % of the continent height scale plus the hill height, whichever is greater.
- **Starting and changing the terrain:** the quadtree is made once a probe has returned the site's natural height, which becomes the pad's height. Changing a parameter (**N**, **M**, **J**, **H**, **G**) releases every chunk, measures the site again and builds the planet again.
- **The ship** ([`flight.py`](flight.py)) is a rigid body in doubles: position, velocity, an orientation quaternion and an angular velocity, with a camera's body axes (forward −Z, up +Y). Its view is the camera's in ship mode. Every frame the time since the last (times the warp) is flown in steps of 1/120 s, at most 64 a frame, with semi-implicit Euler (velocity first, then position).
  - Forces: gravity GM/r² towards the centre (4.905 m/s² at the radius, half of Earth's); drag ½ρv²·C<sub>d</sub>A against the velocity (C<sub>d</sub>A = 12 m²); lift ½ρv<sub>f</sub>|v<sub>f</sub>|·C<sub>L</sub>A along the ship's up for the speed v<sub>f</sub> along its nose (C<sub>L</sub>A = 8 m²); the thrusters: 600 kN forward, 150 kN in each other direction. The air's density is 1.225 kg/m³ × e<sup>−h/2.5 km</sup> (the atmosphere's scale height), times the atmosphere's density factor. Mass 20 t.
  - Rotation assist (always): the stick asks for rates up to 0.8, 0.6 and 1.5 rad/s of pitch, yaw and roll, which the torque (200 kN m at most per axis) pushes towards.
  - Translation assist (**L**): the throttle asks for a speed along the nose (up to 1,500 m/s) and **E / Q** for a climb rate (up to 60 m/s); the thrusters push towards that velocity and hold the ship against gravity, drag and lift, within their limits. Over the ground patch it descends no faster than √(3² + 2 · 1.5 · h) m/s with the gear h metres above the ground: braking at 1.5 m/s² to 3 m/s at the ground, so holding **Q** lands without crashing. Off, the throttle drives the main engine and **E / Q** the vertical thrusters.
  - Ground contact ([`ground.py`](ground.py), `Ship.step`): the ship's shape is the F-117 model's: three gear points 1.95 m below its centre at its wheels (one 4.35 m forward, two 1.15 m back and 2.14 m to the sides) and seven hull points (the nose 9.5 m forward, the tail 8.7 m back, the wing tips 6.6 m out, the cockpit's roof and the two fins' tops). Its ground is a patch of 13 × 13 terrain heights in the plane tangent to the body under the ship, relative to that body's centre (2.4 m apart when the ship is still), interpolated bilinearly, on the planet with the sea as a floor at sea level. The patch is measured around where the ship will be when the heights come back: ahead along its velocity over the ground (less the body's) by the probes' latency times the frame time and the warp. It reaches that distance again either way beyond the ship's 10.3 m reach plus 4 m. Points touching the surface, or reaching it within the step, get impulses along the surface's normal and friction impulses (at most 0.6 times the normal impulse), solved over 8 iterations. They turn the ship as well as move it, so it settles on a slope on its gear. A point reaching the surface faster than 10 m/s (gear) or 3 m/s (hull) crashes the ship: it stops and no longer flies until **8** or **9** gives a new one.
  - A moving surface (`orbits.Planet.surface_motion`): within the moon's sphere of influence the ground moves with the moon (288 m/s and 0.14 m/s² towards the planet). Translation assist asks for velocities relative to it and makes up its acceleration; contacts, crash speeds and friction use velocities relative to it; the ship's position moves by its velocity relative to the surface times the step plus the moon's own displacement over the step, so a ship at rest stays where it is; a crashed ship moves with it. On the planet the surface is still. The instruments and the HUD show speed and climb relative to the body under the ship.
  - Resting: when the gear touches with the throttle at 0, no upward thrust and the stick let go, the thrusters and the torque switch off and the ship settles under gravity; they come back when any of those controls moves.
  - A circular orbit at 40 km is 592 m/s, about 25 minutes round.
- **The moon** (`orbits.Moon`): 25 km in radius with a fifth of the planet's surface gravity, on a circular orbit 600 km from the planet's centre in the plane of the site's up and east (it passes over the site moving east, like preset 9's orbit), at the rate for both masses (3.64 hours round). It starts 0.6 radians east of the site's zenith, and moves with the flight's clock, which runs (times the warp) in free-camera mode too.
  - Everything is reckoned from the planet's centre, so the moon's pull on the ship is its pull less its pull on the planet (the frame's own acceleration): what is left is its tide, about 100 m over a 40 km orbit.
  - Within its sphere of influence (104 km from its centre, its distance times the mass ratio to the power 0.4) orbits are reckoned around the moon, elsewhere around the planet (`orbits.reference`): the orbit path, the periapsis and apoapsis on the HUD and the ORBIT display, whose title names the body, and its circular speed.
  - From the 40 km orbit a Hohmann transfer takes 162 m/s prograde and 53 minutes (3.3 at 16×), with the moon leading by 180° less what it travels in that time.
