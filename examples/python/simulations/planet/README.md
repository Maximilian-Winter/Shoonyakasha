# Planet

A planet of radius 100 km with procedural terrain (continents, oceans, ridged mountains, hills) on a cube-sphere with quadtree level of detail, an ocean and an atmosphere, flown around from a few metres above its ground to 5,000 km away. Its chunks are built on the GPU by a compute pass and drawn with indirect draws. It runs on its own [pipeline](pipeline/pipeline.json) with reverse-Z depth, HDR, an atmosphere pass, a wireframe pass switched on with **F**, and a canvas overlay. Needs numpy and a GPU with 64-bit float shaders (`shaderFloat64`).

```
python planet.py
python planet.py --capture DIR     # screenshots at the presets, then exit
python planet.py --verify          # check the GPU's chunks against cube_sphere.chunk_mesh, then exit
python planet.py --orbit-check     # fly one orbit from preset 9 at 16x, report the drift, then exit
python planet.py --landing-check   # land the ship on a mountain near the site, report how it stands, then exit
python planet.py --crack-check     # measure the gaps between levels in steep terrain against the skirts, then exit
python planet.py --morph-check     # come down from 20 km, check every swap of chunks happens fully morphed, then exit
```

## How it works

- **Positions** are Python floats (64-bit), with the planet's centre at the origin. The engine's camera stays at its own origin and only turns. Every frame each chunk and entity is placed at its position minus the camera's, so the GPU only sees numbers that are small near the camera.
- **The projection** is reverse-Z with an infinite far plane (`scene.set_camera_reverse_z`): depth 1 at the 5 cm near plane and 0 at infinity. The passes compare with `greater_or_equal`, and the terrain pass clears depth to 0.
- **The sphere** ([`cube_sphere.py`](cube_sphere.py)) has six cube faces. Each face has a frame (normal, u axis, v axis) with u × v = normal, and a point normal + u·u_axis + v·v_axis, with u and v in [−1, 1], is mapped onto the sphere by `spherify`. A chunk is a square of a face at a quadtree level: 16 × 16 quads, its vertices relative to its own centre.
- **The terrain** ([`terrain.glsl`](pipeline/shaders/terrain.glsl)) is a height in metres above the sphere for each direction from the planet's centre: domain-warped fBm continents (below zero is sea floor, down to 1,500 m), ridged mountains in bands where a low-frequency mask allows them (3,000 m), hills (150 m), and detail on land from wavelengths of 80 m down to 10 m (15 m in the mountains' bands, 35 % of that outside them), all from 3D gradient noise that hashes integer lattice points. The hills and the detail are eroded fBm: each octave is divided by 1 + erosion × the squared length of the noise gradients summed over the octaves before it (erosion 0.5), so slopes stay smooth and the small features gather on flats and crests. Its parameters are custom values (`planet.terrain.*`) read by the `TerrainParams` uniform buffer. Around the blocks' site the ground is flat within 150 m, at the site's natural height, easing back into the natural terrain by 600 m.
- **Skirts:** each chunk's edge vertices have copies 5 % of the chunk's edge length lower, joined to the edge by walls drawn from both sides. They hide the cracks where chunks of different levels meet.
- **Building chunks on the GPU** ([`chunk_build.comp`](pipeline/shaders/chunk_build.comp)): `ChunkPool` is a storage buffer of 2048 slots of 357 vertices (the 17 × 17 grid, then 4 × 17 skirt vertices), each four `vec4`s: position and u, normal and v, direction and height, and the sine of the horizon towards the sun (see Shadows). A chunk the quadtree wants takes a free slot and a job. Each frame Python sets up to 16 jobs (face, level, x, y, slot, skirt depth, stamp) as custom values, and the `BuildChunks` pass builds one job per workgroup of 64 threads; workgroups past the job count return at once.
  - Face coordinates, `spherify`, and the position direction × (radius + height) minus the chunk's centre are worked out in 64-bit floats and stored as 32-bit. Heights are 32-bit.
  - The grid is built first, then its lowest and highest heights, then the skirts: they reach below their edge by the job's skirt depth (5 % of the chunk's edge) or the grid's height range, whichever is greater. A coarser neighbour's edge is a straight line between vertices of the finer chunk's own grid, so it cannot stand further from the finer edge than that range.
  - A vertex's normal comes from the terrain at four points a short arc away, along two tangents that depend on its direction alone, so a vertex shared by chunks has the same normal in each.
  - Each slot's lowest and highest grid heights go to `ChunkHeights` with the job's stamp, read back every frame. Python gives them to the chunk's quadtree node.
  - A 17th workgroup evaluates the terrain at 128 probe directions into `ProbeHeights`, read back every frame: the ground under the camera, the site's natural height, in ship mode the 121 points of the ground patch around the ship, and in the last four any directions a check asks for. Each result carries the stamp of the frame that asked for it; results asked for with an older terrain version are ignored. The frames between asking and reading back are the probes' latency.
  - A chunk is drawn in the frame its job runs, since `BuildChunks` comes first. Releasing a chunk frees its slot.
- **Drawing** (`Terrain`): one `draw_indexed_indirect` call with a count. Every chunk shares the 2304 indices of `ChunkIndices`, set once at start. Each frame Python writes `ChunkDraws`, the number of chunks to draw and then one command per chunk with `vertexOffset` at its slot's first vertex and `firstInstance` its slot, and `ChunkInstances`, each slot's centre minus the camera's position (subtracted in double) and its colour. [`terrain.vert`](pipeline/shaders/terrain.vert) reads the vertices from `ChunkPool` as its vertex buffer and adds `ChunkInstances[gl_InstanceIndex]`. The blocks are entities, drawn after it by `Blocks`.
- **Colours** ([`terrain.frag`](pipeline/shaders/terrain.frag)): sea floor darker with depth; sand below 40 m; dry ground, grass or forest by a moisture noise; rock where the slope is steep; snow above a snow line that falls from 2,500 m at the equator to 200 m at the poles. The other colour modes show one colour per cube face or per quadtree level, with lines at every power-of-two subdivision of a face ([`shading.glsl`](pipeline/shaders/shading.glsl)).
- **Lighting** ([`atmosphere.glsl`](pipeline/shaders/atmosphere.glsl)): the terrain and the blocks are diffuse surfaces lit by the sun, with an illuminance of 20 dimmed by the air between the surface and the sun (from the transmittance table below; none in the planet's shadow), and a bluish sky light that fades as the sun sets. They are drawn into `hdrColor`, a 16-bit float image. The sun's direction is set by its elevation and azimuth seen from the blocks.
- **Shadows** ([`shadows.py`](shadows.py), [`shadow_terrain.vert`](pipeline/shaders/shadow_terrain.vert), [`shadow_entity.vert`](pipeline/shaders/shadow_entity.vert), [`shadow.glsl`](pipeline/shaders/shadow.glsl)): the terrain, the blocks and the cockpit cast the sun's shadow on the terrain, the sea, the blocks and the cockpit, through four cascades that split the view at 12 m, 150 m, 2 km and 20 km (texels of about 1.7 cm, 21 cm, 2.8 m and 28 m).
  - Each frame Python fits each cascade in doubles: an orthographic window along the sun around its slice's bounding sphere (the same size whichever way the camera turns), reaching 5 km further towards the sun so what stands between it and the sun casts into it, snapped to whole texels in planet space so a fixed point keeps its place in its texel as the camera moves. The matrices map positions relative to the camera to the map (`ShadowParams`).
  - `ShadowTerrain0` to `ShadowTerrain3` (one pass repeated, the cascade from `pass.repeatIndex`) draw every selected chunk within 25 km, in view or not, morphed as drawn, depth only into a 2048² layer each of `sunShadowMap` (slope-scaled depth bias, depth clamped). `ShadowEntities0` to `ShadowEntities3` then add the entities on render layers 1 and 2 (the blocks, the cockpit and its displays), unculled, by their world matrices (`ShadowEntityDraw`).
  - A surface picks its cascade by its distance along the view, moves two of the cascade's texels along its normal, and compares with the map at nine places a texel apart, each a filtered comparison. The shadow fades out over the last 2 km; beyond 20 km the map shadows nothing. Off with the menu's **Shadows**, and while the sun is below the camera's horizon.
  - Distant terrain's shadow, at any distance: each chunk vertex holds the sine of the terrain's horizon towards the sun's azimuth, measured when the chunk is built (`chunk_build.comp`): the highest elevation of the terrain (the sea as a floor) at 20 distances along the ground that way, spaced evenly in their logarithm from two of the chunk's vertex spacings (at least 300 m) out to 40 km. It morphs with the vertex. A surface is lit where the sun stands above that horizon, softened over about 1° near the camera and up to about 4° beyond 60 km, where the vertices are kilometres apart. The terrain and the sea take the lesser of this and the map's share; the blocks take the map's.
  - When the sun has moved more than 0.5° from the direction the horizons were measured towards (`ChunkJobs.horizonSun`), every built chunk is built again in its own slot, nearest first, in the jobs left over by new chunks, keeping its old vertices until then.
  - The sunlight is scaled by the share lit; the sky light is not.
- **The ocean** (`Ocean`, [`ocean.vert`](pipeline/shaders/ocean.vert), [`ocean.frag`](pipeline/shaders/ocean.frag)): the selected chunks whose height range reaches below 0, drawn again from `ChunkPool`, morphed as the terrain is, with each vertex moved along its direction to sea level (its position minus direction × height), through a second draw list, `OceanDraws`. Where the ground is above the sea it hides the water by the depth test, which draws the coastlines. The water depth comes from the terrain function at each pixel (`terrainSeaFloor`: only the continent noise and the seabed away from the coasts, the whole terrain near them). The surface is blended over the sea floor already drawn (premultiplied alpha):
  - reflected light, by Schlick's Fresnel with water's F0 of 0.02: the sky (the sky light over π) and the sun's highlight (normalised Blinn-Phong, with the sunlight that comes through the air). Its exponent is 400 within 3 km of the camera and falls geometrically to 30 by 20 km, so far water reflects the sun as a wider glint, for the waves too small to draw there;
  - light that enters the water and is scattered back out of it, with the sea floor showing through 1/e as much per 12 m of water the view crosses;
  - ripples 20 m across that move with time and tilt the normal, fading out by 3 km from the camera.
  The ocean writes depth, so the atmosphere is laid over the sea surface, not the sea floor.
- **The atmosphere** (`Atmosphere`, [`atmosphere.frag`](pipeline/shaders/atmosphere.frag)): a fullscreen pass that reads `hdrColor` and the depth buffer and writes the swapchain. For each pixel it builds the view ray from the inverse projection and view, takes the distance to the scene from the reverse-Z depth (0 is the sky), and raymarches the light scattered towards the camera from where the ray enters the atmosphere to the scene or its exit: 32 steps, denser near the start. Each step adds sunlight scattered once by air (Rayleigh) and haze (Mie, g = 0.8), with the sun's transmittance from the transmittance table (none in the planet's shadow), and light scattered more than once, from the multiple scattering table. The scene is seen through the transmittance, plus the scattered light; the sky gets a sun disk.
  - Two lookup tables (Hillaire 2020), built every frame by compute passes before the terrain (`TransmittanceLut`, `MultiScatteringLut`, [`transmittance_lut.comp`](pipeline/shaders/transmittance_lut.comp), [`multi_scattering_lut.comp`](pipeline/shaders/multi_scattering_lut.comp)) into 16-bit float images that `cameraSet` samples linearly:
    - transmittance, 256 × 64: from a height towards the top of the atmosphere along a direction, for directions that clear the planet, in 40 steps;
    - multiple scattering, 32 × 32 by height and the sun's zenith angle: over 64 directions around a point, the sunlight scattered once towards it (by air, haze and ground with an albedo of 0.3) and the share of light the air scatters, summed over all further orders as L₂ / (1 − f<sub>ms</sub>).
  - Ozone absorbs without scattering, in a layer that peaks at 7.8 km and falls to nothing 4.7 km above and below, with Earth's absorption times 3.2 (like the air's). The menu's **Multiple scattering** and **Ozone** switch them off for comparison. The result is scaled by the exposure and tone mapped (`ACESFilm` from `sk/tonemap.glsl`).
  - The atmosphere's top is 20 km up; the air's and the haze's densities fall off with scale heights of 2.5 km and 400 m. Their coefficients are Earth's times 3.2, which keeps Earth's optical depth straight up; **T / Y** scale them. They are custom values (`planet.atmosphere.*`) read by `AtmosphereParams`.
  - Ray and sphere intersections work from the ray's closest point to the planet's centre, which keeps them to about half a metre with the camera 5,000 km out.
  - The camera's position relative to the planet's centre (`planet.cameraFromCentre`, from the doubles) places the rays and the lit surfaces.
- **Clouds** (`Clouds`, [`clouds.frag`](pipeline/shaders/clouds.frag), [`clouds.glsl`](pipeline/shaders/clouds.glsl)): a layer between 1.5 and 3.5 km up.
  - Density: a coverage noise on the direction from the planet's centre (cells of about 2.5 km), raised so that the menu's **Cloud cover** share of the sky is cloud; cut down by a profile over the layer's height (rising over its lowest tenth, falling over its upper half) so only the densest weather reaches its bottom and top, which gives flat bases and rounded tops; eroded at the edges by a 3D detail noise (900 m). The extinction at full density is the menu's **Cloud density** (0.04 per metre).
  - A fullscreen pass at a quarter of the screen's resolution each way marches each view ray through the layer, up to the scene's depth and at most 50 km of the layer: 64 steps spaced with the square of their index (short near the camera), shifted by a per-pixel, per-frame random amount. At each step the sunlight comes through the atmosphere (the transmittance table) and through the cloud towards the sun (4 steps out to 1.2 km); light scattered many times is approximated by four octaves, each with 0.3 times the extinction, half the anisotropy of the two-lobed Henyey-Greenstein phase function and half the weight of the last; light from all around adds the sky's and 5 % of the sunlight above the clouds (scattered through the whole layer, it lights even their depths), both brighter towards the layer's top. It writes the clouds' light and transmittance (`cloudLayer`) and their mean distance with the scene's distance it marched to (`cloudDistance`).
  - The atmosphere pass takes the four nearest cloud texels, weighted bilinearly and by how close their scene distance is to the pixel's (so clouds do not bleed over nearer edges), and puts together the air in front of the clouds, the clouds seen through it, and the scene and the air behind them through the clouds.
  - Their shadows: the terrain, the sea and the blocks scale the sunlight by the clouds' transmittance towards the sun, from their density at four points along the sun's direction through the layer (at most 6 km of it), plus 30 % of what the clouds stop, which reaches the ground scattered through them.
  - Wind: each frame the coverage noise moves by the menu's **Wind** speed (15 m/s) times the frame's time and the time warp; the detail noise moves with it, at the same speed in metres.
  - The menu's **Clouds** switches them off.
- **Level of detail** ([`quadtree.py`](quadtree.py)): each face is the root of a quadtree, down to level 12 (chunks 38 m across, quads 2.4 m). Every frame:
  - A node splits into its four children when the camera is within the split factor (1.25) times its edge length of its bounding sphere. A split node merges again only beyond 1.25 times that.
  - A node's bounding sphere holds its patch of the sphere raised and lowered through its height range, plus 5 % of its edge length. The range is the one its chunk's heights gave, or its parent's until they come back; the roots start with −2,000 to 6,000 m.
  - A node stays drawn until all four of its children are built, so there are no holes.
  - Morphing ([`morph.glsl`](pipeline/shaders/morph.glsl), in the terrain's and the ocean's vertex shaders, which read the ancestors' vertices from `ChunkPool`): a chunk's ancestor k levels up has its vertices at every 2<sup>k</sup>-th vertex of the chunk (k up to 4), split along the same diagonals, so its surface at any vertex is a weighted sum of three of the chunk's vertices (`cube_sphere.stride_weights`). Each vertex blends towards its parent's surface, then from there towards its grandparent's, and so on, by smoothstep(0.8 S<sub>k</sub>, S<sub>k</sub>, its distance from the camera), S<sub>k</sub> the k-th ancestor's split distance (the parent's, `ChunkInstances.morph.x`, times 2<sup>k−1</sup>). A parent splits when the camera comes within S<sub>1</sub> of its bounding sphere, which holds the chunk's vertices, so a chunk appears and goes fully blended to its parent's surface; an edge beside a coarser neighbour lies on the neighbour's grid and follows its blending. Normals, directions and heights blend alike; skirts keep their offset from their edge. The ground patch (physics) keeps the true terrain.
  - At most 16 missing chunks are built per frame: those in view first, nearest first. None are built while the pool is full.
  - Chunks no frame has used for 120 frames are released.
  - Of the chunks the tree selects, those whose bounding sphere is out of view or behind the horizon are not drawn (nor redrawn by the ocean or the wireframe). In view: within a cone around the view direction that holds the screen's diagonal. Behind the horizon ([`quadtree.py`](quadtree.py) `hidden_by_horizon`): wholly inside the cone from the camera tangent to a sphere at the terrain's floor and beyond the circle where that cone touches it. The floor is the deepest the terrain can go: 10 % more than the ocean depth, or than 5 % of the continent height scale plus the hill height, whichever is greater.
- **Starting and changing the terrain:** the quadtree is made once a probe has returned the site's natural height, which becomes the pad's height. Changing a parameter (**N**, **M**, **J**, **H**, **G**) releases every chunk, measures the site again and builds the planet again.
- **The ship** ([`flight.py`](flight.py)) is a rigid body in doubles: position, velocity, an orientation quaternion and an angular velocity, with a camera's body axes (forward −Z, up +Y). Its view is the camera's in ship mode. Every frame the time since the last (times the warp) is flown in steps of 1/120 s, at most 64 a frame, with semi-implicit Euler (velocity first, then position).
  - Forces: gravity GM/r² towards the centre (4.905 m/s² at the radius, half of Earth's); drag ½ρv²·C<sub>d</sub>A against the velocity (C<sub>d</sub>A = 12 m²); lift ½ρv<sub>f</sub>|v<sub>f</sub>|·C<sub>L</sub>A along the ship's up for the speed v<sub>f</sub> along its nose (C<sub>L</sub>A = 8 m²); the thrusters: 600 kN forward, 150 kN in each other direction. The air's density is 1.225 kg/m³ × e<sup>−h/2.5 km</sup> (the atmosphere's scale height), times the atmosphere's density factor. Mass 20 t.
  - Rotation assist (always): the stick asks for rates up to 0.8, 0.6 and 1.5 rad/s of pitch, yaw and roll, which the torque (200 kN m at most per axis) pushes towards.
  - Translation assist (**L**): the throttle asks for a speed along the nose (up to 1,500 m/s) and **E / Q** for a climb rate (up to 60 m/s); the thrusters push towards that velocity and hold the ship against gravity, drag and lift, within their limits. Over the ground patch it descends no faster than √(3² + 2 · 1.5 · h) m/s with the gear h metres above the ground: braking at 1.5 m/s² to 3 m/s at the ground, so holding **Q** lands without crashing. Off, the throttle drives the main engine and **E / Q** the vertical thrusters.
  - Ground contact ([`ground.py`](ground.py), `Ship.step`): the ship's shape is three gear points 2 m below its centre (one 4 m forward, two 2.5 m back and 2.5 m to the sides) and five hull points (nose, tail, wing tips 6 m out, roof). Its ground is a patch of 11 × 11 terrain heights in the plane tangent to the planet (2.4 m apart when the ship is still), interpolated bilinearly, with the sea as a floor at sea level. The patch is measured around where the ship will be when the heights come back: ahead along its velocity over the ground by the probes' latency times the frame time and the warp. It reaches that distance again either way beyond the ship's 8 m reach plus 4 m. Points touching the surface, or reaching it within the step, get impulses along the surface's normal and friction impulses (at most 0.6 times the normal impulse), solved over 8 iterations. They turn the ship as well as move it, so it settles on a slope on its gear. A point reaching the surface faster than 10 m/s (gear) or 3 m/s (hull) crashes the ship: it stops and no longer flies until **8** or **9** gives a new one.
  - Resting: when the gear touches with the throttle at 0, no upward thrust and the stick let go, the thrusters and the torque switch off and the ship settles under gravity; they come back when any of those controls moves.
  - A circular orbit at 40 km is 592 m/s, about 25 minutes round.
- **The cockpit** ([`cockpit.py`](cockpit.py)): boxes and beams in the camera's space (a dashboard, side consoles, canopy pillars, a roof bow, rails and sills, the displays' bezels), one mesh drawn at the camera with its rotation, so it moves with the view. It is lit like the blocks and drawn into the HDR image before the atmosphere, which adds nothing visible over a metre.
- **The head-up display** shows in ship mode:
  - Symbols ([`hud.frag`](pipeline/shaders/hud.frag), the `HudSymbols` pass after `Atmosphere`, added over the image): each pixel is tested by the direction it looks in, so the symbols sit at infinity and stay on what they mark as the ship rolls. The horizon, square to the planet's up; a pitch ladder every 10°, beside the nose's heading and within 35° of its pitch, dashed below the horizon; a ring where the ship is moving; a cross at the nose. Their directions come from `HudParams` (`planet.hud.*`).
  - Readouts ([`instruments.py`](instruments.py), a screen canvas): speed, with the throttle, assist and time warp and a throttle bar under it, on the left; height above the ground or the sea on the right; the heading at the top.
  - Two displays on the dashboard, world canvases on render layer 2, drawn by the `Screens` pass ([`screen.frag`](pipeline/shaders/screen.frag)) from their `emissiveMap`: FLIGHT (speed, climb rate, heights, throttle, assist) and ORBIT (periapsis, apoapsis, speed and the circular speed at this height, the air's density, the dynamic pressure and the reentry glow).
  - The reentry glow (`ReentryGlow`, [`reentry.frag`](pipeline/shaders/reentry.frag), a fullscreen pass after `Atmosphere`, added over the image, enabled while there is a glow): `flight.reentry_glow` gives its strength from 0 to 1. It is not a physical temperature but the product of the speed's rise from 0.6 to 0.85 of the circular speed at the surface (700 m/s) and the air density's rise, in powers of ten, from 10⁻⁵ to 10⁻³ of sea level's. A ship coming down from orbit glows from about 25 km until the air has slowed it; flight at 300 m/s near the ground does not. The pass tests each pixel's direction: a small white-pink core along the direction of travel, and an orange sheath of thin streaks flowing back from about 18° off it, flickering; pixels nearer than 20 m (the cockpit) get none. The blocks and the cockpit are on layer 1, drawn by `Blocks`.
- **The orbit path** (`OrbitPath`, [`orbit.vert`](pipeline/shaders/orbit.vert), [`orbit.frag`](pipeline/shaders/orbit.frag)), whenever there is a ship: `flight.orbit_path` gives 256 points of the conic the ship would coast along, from the ship onwards, evenly spaced in eccentric anomaly around an ellipse, or on an escape out to 10 times its distance from the centre. Each frame they go to `OrbitPoints` relative to the camera (subtracted in double) and are drawn as a 2-pixel line strip by an indexed indirect draw after `Atmosphere`, over the tone-mapped image, depth-tested against the scene so the planet hides what lies behind it. The line fades in between 200 m and 2 km from the camera.
- **The settings menu** (**F1**, [`menu.py`](menu.py), a screen canvas down the right side): the time warp, translation assist, exposure, atmosphere density, multiple scattering, ozone, the sun's elevation and azimuth, the split factor, the ocean, the orbit path, shadows, clouds with their cover, density and wind, wireframe and debug text, and the colour mode, all taking effect at once; and the planet's seed (a new one at once), mountain and hill heights, continent frequency, sea level bias, ocean depth, detail height and erosion, which **Apply** builds the planet with. It follows changes made with the keys. Its widgets are left out of keyboard navigation, so Tab and the arrow keys keep flying.
- **The wireframe pass** draws the chunks again in line mode, with a slope-scaled depth bias (`"depthBias": {"slope": 1.5}`; positive is nearer with reverse-Z) so the lines pass the depth test against their own surface.
- **The blocks** stand on the pad on the +Z face at u = v = 1/64: a 20 m slab 5 cm above the ground, cubes of 0.5, 1 and 4 m, and a 30 m mast.

`tests/python/test_planet_cockpit.py` checks the cockpit's winding and that its displays face the eye. `tests/python/test_planet_flight.py` checks the flight model: quaternions and axes, a circular orbit held for five minutes, orbit elements, the orbit path (a circle, an ellipse between its apsides in the plane of the motion, an escape out to its reach, a thin ellipse for a slow, nearly vertical descent, none for a radial motion), the reentry glow (none in orbit or at 300 m/s near the ground; brightest between 25 and 8 km coming down from orbit), a scripted climb from the ground to orbit, free fall, terminal speed and drag, assisted hover and speed, the rotation assist, and ground contact: settling on level ground and on a 20° slope, sliding to a stop, landing with assist and resting, landing from up to 1.5 km holding full downward thrust, assist's full descent rate high above the ground, lifting off, crashing too fast on the gear or the hull, and not passing through the ground at 3 km/s. `tests/python/test_planet_ground.py` checks the ground patch's grid, its heights and normals on level and sloping ground, the sea floor, and points outside it. `tests/python/test_planet_shadows.py` checks the cascades: every point of a slice in its window and depth, casters towards the sun within reach in depth and nearer, depth along the sun alone, a planet point keeping its place in its texel as the camera moves, the windows' sizes unchanged as the camera turns, and growing with distance. `--orbit-check` flies one orbit in the running example and exits with status 1 if the heights left 100 m around 40 km. `--landing-check` finds the highest of 64 spots 10 to 60 km around the site, comes down on it from 500 m with assist and full downward thrust, and checks that it touches down under 5 m/s and that every gear point rests within 5 cm of both the patch and the terrain probed at its own direction; then it dives a ship at the spot at 400 m/s and 16× warp and checks that it crashes with its centre at least 1 m above the ground. It exits with status 1 if any of these fails. `tests/python/test_planet_cube_sphere.py` checks the face frames, `spherify`, the winding, the skirts, that neighbouring chunks share their edge vertices exactly, that the pipeline's chunk buffers fit the chunk layout, and the quadtree's selection, hysteresis, build order, bounds through a height range, eviction, horizon culling (against brute force: every point of each hidden sphere is behind the occluder), `face_uv`, the inverse of `direction` on every face up to the corners, and the morph's weights: the rising diagonal in every quad, every odd vertex at the middle of a parent's edge, and coarser strides nesting. `--crack-check` settles at six views (presets 1, 2 and 6, beside and over a peak, and over it with the mountains twice as high), reads `ChunkPool` back, and for every selected chunk's edge whose neighbour is coarser measures the gap at each edge vertex to the neighbour's straight edge, against the skirt that has to cover it (the finer chunk's where its edge is higher, the coarser chunk's where it is lower). It measures the gaps again as drawn, with both chunks' vertices morphed as `morph.glsl` morphs them (the coarser chunk's found by position), also in pixels. It prints per view the largest gap, as a share of its skirt, unmorphed and as drawn, and exits with status 1 if a share is above 1. `--morph-check` waits until the view 20 km above the site is built, then brings the camera down to 100 m over 2,400 frames, moving north at a third of its height per second. Each frame it finds the chunks that appeared because their parent split or went because they merged, and the least morph factor their vertices had then (from the bounding sphere's distance); swaps whose children were built only after the camera passed the split distance count as late. It exits with status 1 if any swap was late or a factor was below 0.99. `--verify` reads `ChunkPool` back and checks every built chunk: directions and face coordinates equal to `cube_sphere.chunk_mesh`'s; positions equal to direction × (radius + height − skirt) − centre from its directions and the GPU's heights, to within one unit in the last place of the chunk's largest coordinate; normals of unit length on the outer side; and the lowest and highest heights in `ChunkHeights` equal to those in the pool. It exits with status 1 if any of these fails.
