# Planets: `shoonyakasha.planet`

A planet and its moon with procedural terrain on the planet pipeline (`shoonyakasha/pipelines/planet/`): a cube-sphere with quadtree level of detail whose chunks a compute pass builds, an atmosphere, clouds, an ocean, sun shadows and ground textures. The [planet example](../../../examples/python/simulations/planet/README.md) flies a ship over it; the [planet explorer](../../../examples/python/simulations/planet_explorer/README.md) looks at it like a globe or a planetarium. Needs numpy, Pillow (which bakes the vegetation's textures), and a GPU with 64-bit float shaders (`shaderFloat64`).

## A program on a `PlanetWorld`

```python
import shoonyakasha as sk
from shoonyakasha.planet import cameras, world as planet_world

planet_world.compile_shaders()
engine = sk.Engine(width=1600, height=900, pipeline_json_path=str(planet_world.PIPELINE))
world = planet_world.PlanetWorld(engine)
camera = None


def frame(dt):
    world.begin_frame(camera.position)
    world.time += dt
    camera.update(dt, active=not engine.ui.pointer_over_ui)
    world.end_frame(camera.position, camera.basis, dt)
    return True


def on_init():
    global camera
    world.setup()
    camera = cameras.GlobeCamera(world, range_=300_000.0)
    engine.ecs.add_system("Planet", frame, priority=-1)


engine.set_on_init(on_init)
engine.input.set_on_mouse_scroll(lambda x, y: camera.scroll(y))
engine.run()
```

`compile_shaders()` compiles the pipeline's shaders that changed since their SPIR-V was built; without glslc it leaves the SPIR-V shipped with the package.

## `PlanetWorld(engine, fov=70.0, near=0.05, terrain=None, moon=True, pad=True)`

Positions are Python floats in metres, with the planet's centre at the origin. The engine's camera (`camera`, made by `setup`) stays at its own origin and only turns; every chunk is placed at its position less the camera's, in double. The projection is reverse-Z with an infinite far plane.

|---|---|
| `setup()` | From `on_init`: the camera entity, the sky, clouds and terrain settings, the chunks' index buffer, the ground's textures (when downloaded), the vegetation's textures, impostors and meshes (the rocks' too) and, with `moon`, the moon |

| `set_orbit_path(points)` | A line through up to `ORBIT_POINTS` points (planet space) over the scene, depth-tested; none when empty |

| `set_craters(craters)` | The craters, up to `MAX_CRATERS` `Crater(body, direction, radius, depth, rim)`: a bowl `depth` metres deep at the unit `direction` from body index `body`'s centre, reaching the ground at `radius` metres, with a rim `rim` metres high fading out by `CRATER_REACH` radii, added to the ground after the flat sites; the scatter places nothing within the radius (`craters.glsl`). When they change and the planet's tree is made, the planet is built again; `regenerate` drops them |
| `set_rivers(rivers, frame=None)` | The planet's rivers, `River(points, levels, widths, depths)` each, over the `rivers.GridFrame` they were traced over: their centre lines through the unit directions `points`, with the water's level (never rising along a river), the width and the depth at the centre line at each. Within half a river's width of its centre line the ground is a channel under the water; beyond it the ground eases from the water level into the planet's own over a bank 8 m wide plus 2.5 m per metre the ground stands above the water, at most 120 m, and never rises. The Rivers pass draws the water's mesh (with the ocean, `set_ocean`), the ground under it takes the sea floor's colours, and the scatter places nothing in it (`rivers.glsl`). At most `rivers.MAX_DATA` vec4 of river data and `rivers.MAX_VERTICES` vertices of water. When they change and the planet's tree is made, the planet is built again; `regenerate` drops them |
| `water_level(direction)` | The water level over the unit `direction` on the planet: a lake's (`lake_level`) or, where a river's water covers it, the river's, or None |
| `request_height_grid(frame)`, `height_grid()` | Measure the planet's terrain, with its lakes' water as its surface and its flat sites, at the directions of a `rivers.GridFrame` (at most `HEIGHT_GRID_POINTS`, 512 × 512) on the GPU (the `HeightGrid` pass); `height_grid()` gives the (size, size) heights, row by row along east, once they are back, else None |

| `body_under(point)` | The body `point` is reckoned around: the moon within its sphere of influence, else the planet |
| `altitude(point)`, `height_above_ground(point)`, `surface_height(point)`, `datum_name(point)` | Heights above the body's sphere (sea level on the planet) and above the ground measured under the viewpoint (the sea where that is below sea level) |

| `site_frame()`, `site_ground()`, `sun_direction()` | The site on the +Z face (its point, up, east and north), its point on the ground, and the sun's direction from its elevation and azimuth seen there |

The terrain's parameters, the atmosphere, the clouds, the shadow cascades and the pool's sizes are the module's constants (`TERRAIN`, whose `gullies` are their depth (0 for none), wavelength, octaves and the slope at which they reach full depth; `RADIUS`, `MAX_LEVEL`, `SPLIT_FACTOR`, `CLOUD_LAYER`, `SHADOW_SPLITS`, `SLOTS`, `MAX_JOBS`, `PROBES` and the others); those the pipeline's buffers and shaders depend on match `pipeline.json` and the shaders. `complete_terrain(terrain)` gives a saved planet's terrain parameters with any it lacks taken from `TERRAIN`. With `pad`, the terrain is flat within 150 m of the site, at its natural height, easing back by 600 m. The flat sites (`set_sites`) flatten either body in the same way, in its chunks, its probes and its collider probes, and the craters (`set_craters`) dig into it in all three and in the height grid.

## `sk.PlanetLod`

The level of detail in C++ (`include/Planet/PlanetLod.h`): per body a quadtree over the six cube faces, the pool's slots and the jobs that build chunks into them (each with a stamp the heights read back carry), the chunks waiting for their heights, the chunks to build again for a new sun, and the draw lists with each slot's instance values. `PlanetWorld` drives it; it does what `quadtree.py` and the pool did in Python, the same to the bit (`tests/python/test_planet_native_lod.py`).

| Call | |
|---|---|
| `PlanetLod(slots, max_jobs, vertices_per_chunk, skirt_fraction, evict_frames=120, merge_ratio=1.25)` | An empty pool of `slots` |
| `create_tree(body, radius, low, high, max_level, split_factor, height_pad)`, `drop_tree(body)`, `has_tree(body)` | A body's tree, its six roots given slots and jobs; dropped with its chunks released |

## Physics

`sk.PlanetPhysics(max_bodies=4096, threads=-1)` holds rigid bodies on Jolt Physics in single precision (`include/Planet/PlanetPhysics.h`), stepped on `threads` worker threads (-1: up to four, one fewer than the hardware has). It has no gravity of its own; moving bodies lose 0.05 of their velocity a second (Jolt's linear damping) until `set_linear_damping` changes it. Positions are metres in the caller's frame, rotations unit quaternions (w, x, y, z), motions `"static"`, `"kinematic"` or `"dynamic"`. Static bodies, ground meshes among them, collide with moving ones only. Ground meshes are fine or coarse, and a moving body near (the start) or far (`set_far`): near bodies collide with the fine ground, far ones with the coarse. Bodies are int ids; a call with an id that is not in the world raises `ValueError`.

Characters are capsules moved by their velocity (Jolt's `CharacterVirtual`), each with its own up, set by the caller. A character's position is the bottom of its capsule; it stands on what it touches below the middle of its lower cap. Each carries a kinematic inner body that moving bodies collide with. Characters have int ids of their own.

`bubble.Bubble(physics, body, gm, origin)` is a frame for those bodies on `body` (a `PlanetWorld` body) whose GM is `gm`. Bubble coordinates are positions relative to the body's centre minus `origin` (body-relative); the axes are the body's, so on the moon the frame moves with it. `to_bubble(point)` and `to_world(p)` convert from and to planet space, `body_relative(p)`, `up(p)` and `gravity(p)` give the point from the body's centre, the way up there and gravity's acceleration. `step(dt)` applies gravity towards the body's centre and steps the physics. `maybe_rebase(focus)` moves the origin to `focus` (bubble coordinates) once it is `REBASE_DISTANCE` (500 m) or more away: `rebase(offset)` shifts every body by −`offset` and calls each of `listeners` with it. `anchor`, `east` and `north` are the frame of the plane the terrain collider's grid lies in; the anchor moves to the origin once the origin is more than `ANCHOR_REACH` (1 km) from it. `RADIUS` is the bubble's reach, 2 km from the origin.

`character.Player(bubble, feet, heading)` is a character (`RADIUS` 0.3 m, `HEIGHT` 1.8 m, `MASS` 80 kg, slopes up to `MAX_SLOPE` 50°) with its feet at bubble coordinates `feet`, facing `heading` made square to the up there. Its up is the way away from the body's centre at its feet; its view is `heading` (along the ground) and `pitch` (radians above it, within ±89°). `step(controls, dt, covered=True)` moves it for one fixed step with a `character.Controls` (`forward` and `strafe` from −1 to 1, `run`, `crouch`, `jump`, taken by the next step on the ground, and `jet`, held): the velocity along the ground, relative to what it stands on, goes towards `WALK`, `RUN` or `CROUCH` (4, 8, 2 m/s) by at most `ACCEL_GROUND` (40 m/s²) or in the air `ACCEL_AIR` (6 m/s²); a jump leaves at `JUMP_SPEED` (3.3 m/s); the jetpack climbs at `JET_ACCEL` (9 m/s²) while `jet_fuel` (0 to 1) lasts, burning `JET_BURN` a second and refilling at `JET_REFILL` on the ground. While not `covered` (by the terrain mesh) it is held where it is (`held`). `look(dx, dy)` turns the view by a mouse movement in pixels (`LOOK_RADIANS_PER_PIXEL`), `push(dv)` adds to its velocity (more than `PUSH_LIFT` upwards lifts it off the ground), `feet()`, `up()`, `eye()` (bubble coordinates; `EYE_HEIGHT` 1.65 m above the feet, `CROUCH_EYE_HEIGHT` 1 m crouched, lowered by a step up and with a head bob of `BOB_AMPLITUDE` every `BOB_STRIDE` metres while `bob`) and `basis()` ((right, up, forward) of the view); `on_ground`, `crouching`, `remove()`.

`collider_probes` builds the terrain under a bubble as a mesh body. `ColliderPatch(body, anchor, east, north, cell, columns=65, spacing=0.5)` is a grid of directions in the plane tangent to the body's sphere at `anchor`, centred on grid cell `cell` of that plane, so patches of one anchor share their directions where they overlap; `set_heights(heights)` (below sea level counts as sea level on a body with a sea), `points()`, `offset(point)`, `covers(point, margin=0.0)`, `triangle(point)` (a corner and the unit normal of the grid triangle under it), `normal(point)` and `height(point)` (where the line from the body's centre through it meets that triangle) take points relative to the body's centre. `ColliderProbes(engine)` hands a patch's directions (up to 4,225) to the pipeline's `ColliderProbes` pass with a stamp (`request(patch, version)`; one a frame: a second request in a frame returns False and asks nothing), takes the heights back by stamp some frames later (`read(version)`, the same patch to every caller in a frame, dropping those asked for under another terrain version), and tells the pass to measure nothing in frames without a request (`end_frame()`). `TerrainCollider(bubble, probes, columns=65, spacing=0.5, friction=0.8, coarse=False)` keeps one patch as a static mesh body (coarse ground with `coarse`); several can share the probes: `update(focus, velocity, latency, version)` builds a patch whose heights came back and asks for a new one, centred ahead of `focus` by `velocity` times `latency` seconds, when that point is more than a quarter of the patch's width from its centre or the terrain version changed; the mesh in use stays until then. `covers(p, margin=0.0)` takes bubble coordinates; `drop()` removes the mesh.

`nearby_trees.NearbyTrees(engine)` lists the trees, cacti and rocks near the camera, which the `VegetationScatter` pass appends to the `NearbyTrees` buffer as it places them (only on that frame's scatter draws: the drawn chunks and those within `SCATTER_NEAR` of the camera or from which a plant or its shadow reaches into view, up to `CAPACITY`, 1,024). `request(camera, radius, centres)` asks for those within `radius` metres of `camera` (planet space) this frame, with a stamp, `centres` being the bodies' centres (planet space, by index) that frame; `read()` gives the list that came back some frames later, or None. Each `NearbyTree` has its `key` ((body, grid, cube face, cell u, cell v), the same every frame), `kind` (a name of `vegetation.KINDS`), `base` (from its body's centre as it was that frame, on the drawn surface), `height`, `width` (the factor `vegetation.glsl` widens it by), `turn` (cosine and sine about its up), `tint`, `grade` (an ore rock's ore richness, 0 for every other) and `resource` (an ore rock's resource id, 0 for every other); `body` is its body's index, `rock` whether it is a rock, and `trunk` (radius, height) of a plant's trunk in metres, from `TRUNKS`, the share of the plant's height its mesh's trunk has. `listed_keys(data)` gives (stamp, keys) of a readback's bytes.

`trunks.Trunks()` keeps a static capsule (friction 0.8) in a bubble for each tree and cactus of a `NearbyTrees` list: `update(bubble, trees, camera, radius)` gives each listed plant on the bubble's body that is not a rock, and has no capsule yet or another kind or a base moved by more than `MOVED` (5 cm), a capsule of its trunk's radius standing along the up from `SINK` (0.5 m) below its base to its trunk's height, and removes those more than `radius` + `KEEP` (10 m) from `camera` (planet space); a list adds capsules and does not remove them. `trunks` maps each key to its `Trunk` (body, kind, base, radius); `clear(bubble)` removes them all.

`wind.wind_at(point, up, time, speed, height)` is the wind's velocity (m/s, planet space) at `point` (from the planet's centre, with unit `up`) `height` metres above the ground, for a wind of `speed` at `REFERENCE_HEIGHT` (1 km): along the ground's part of the prevailing direction (`prevailing(time)`, `BASE_DIRECTION` turned about `TURN_AXIS` once every `TURN_PERIOD`, 6 h), at `speed` times that part's length times `profile(height)` (ln(h / 0.1) / ln(1000 / 0.1), h at least 1 m), plus a gust of `GUST_SHARE` (0.25) of that times value noise (`noise3`) over `GUST_SCALE` (200 m) carried with the wind. `PlanetWorld.wind_at(body, point, height=None)` gives it at the world's clock for `wind_speed` on the planet (`point` from its centre; `height` by default the height above the ground under the viewpoint), and none on the moon. `PlanetWorld.blow_clouds(seconds)` moves the clouds along the prevailing direction at the profile's speed in the middle of the cloud layer (`CLOUD_ROTATION` is clouds.glsl's).

`wind.Storm(centre, axis, speed, radius, start, end, body_radius)` is a moving storm: `centre_at(time)` (its centre moved `speed` m/s along the ground about `axis`), `strength(time)` (0 to 1, rising over `FADE`, 600 s, from `start` and falling over the last `FADE` to `end`) and `at(direction, time)` (the strength inside its radius, falling to 0 across its edge, `EDGE` (a quarter) of the radius). `wind_at`'s `storm` (0 to 1) raises the steady speed by up to `STORM_WIND` (2.5) times and the gusts' share up to `STORM_GUST` (0.5); `PlanetWorld.wind_at` gives it how much of `PlanetWorld.storm` (a `Storm` or None) is there. Each frame `end_frame` calls `upload_storm(position)`: the storm's centre, radius, edge and strength for the clouds, whose cover rises towards `STORM_COVERAGE` (0.95) inside it, and `STORM_HAZE` (1/1,500 per metre) times how much of it is at the camera for the atmosphere pass's grey haze.

`props.Props(engine, mesh, world, on_remove=None)` holds the props (`crates`, `rocks`, `all`), each a `Prop` drawn by a mesh entity and in one of three states: `"jolt"` (a body in the bubble's physics), `"flying"` (out of it, coasting around its body under that body's gravity and, on the planet, the air's drag) and `"resting"` (out of it, where it came down). `mesh` is (positions, normals) of a unit cube centred on the origin, for the crates; `world` is the `PlanetWorld`. `spawn_crate(bubble, position, rotation, size=0.6, density=500.0, colour=CRATE_COLOUR, mass=None)` adds a crate at bubble coordinates; `add_rock(...)` adds a loose rock whose dynamic convex hull and entities the caller made, with its `grade` and `resource` (ferrite for an ore rock given none). Each frame with a bubble, `update(bubble, fine, coarse)` (the fine and coarse `TerrainCollider`s) moves props between the states, `fly(dt, latency)` moves the flying ones on by `dt` seconds of the world's clock, `probe(bubble, probes)` measures the ground under those that need it with up to `probes` of the world's extra probes while nothing else uses them (until then a prop's ground is taken as the top of its body's `heights`), and `draw(bubble, camera)` places the entities relative to `camera` (planet space); each physics step, `drag(bubble)` applies the air's drag to the props in the physics moving faster than `DRAG_SPEED` through the air (their velocity less `PlanetWorld.wind_at`'s), to an asleep one only when the drag is more than `STICK` (0.7) of its weight; the flying ones are dragged through it the same way. The module's docstring gives the rules between the states. `record(bubble, prop)` and `records(bubble)` give props as dicts of plain values (JSON), and `restore(record, bodies, entity=None, points=None, glow=None)` makes one out of the physics from a record (`bodies`: the world bodies by name; a rock needs its entity and its hull's points; an ore rock's record without a resource is ferrite). `find(body)`, `remove(bubble, prop, release=True)` (`on_remove(prop)` is told of a rock with a cell), `clear(bubble)`, `leave_all(bubble)` (every prop out of the physics), `transforms(bubble, props=None)`, `grabbable(physics)` ((body, radius, mass) of each prop in the physics) and `awake(bubble)` (how many are awake). `stack_offsets(count, size)` and `yaw_rotation(right, up, back, angle)` help place crates.

`gravity_gun.Gun(player, objects, params=None, promote=None, too_hot=None)` is the gun of a `character.Player`: `objects()` gives (body, radius, mass) of each body it may grab, `promote(body)` turns one of those that is not dynamic dynamic before the gun acts on it, and `too_hot(body)`, when given, says which of them it may not grab or pull. `step(controls, dt)` runs once per physics step after the player's with a `GunControls` (`grab` held to grab or pull, pressed while holding to drop; `punt`, taken by the next step; `punt_held`, which charges a punt while holding; `rotate`, pixels turning the held body; `distance`, wheel notches changing the hold distance between `MIN_HOLD` and `MAX_HOLD`). A held body is pulled towards a point `hold_distance` plus its radius in front of the eye with its weight cancelled, up to `GunParams.hold_force`; a punt gives a body an impulse along the view of at most `punt_impulse` and `punt_speed`, and the player `recoil_factor` of it back (at most `MAX_RECOIL` m/s). `status` is `"holding"`, `"too_heavy"`, `"too_hot"`, `"can_grab"` or `"idle"`, and `charge` (0 to 1) how far a punt held while holding has charged; `held`, `target`, `hold_distance` and `last_punt` ((body or None, speed given, recoil velocity)) show its state, and `drop()` lets go. `GunParams` holds its ranges, forces and the charged punt's settings; the module's docstring gives the rules.

`on_foot.OnFoot(engine, world, crate_mesh, make_rocks=None, strike_momentum=300.0, strike_reported=100.0)` puts those together for a `PlanetWorld`. It owns `physics` (an `sk.PlanetPhysics` reporting strikes from `strike_reported` kg m/s), `collider_probes`, `nearby_trees`, `trunks` and `props` (crates from `crate_mesh`, (positions, normals) of a unit cube); `make_rocks(props)` gives `rocks`, an object for the scatter's rocks in the bubble (`update`, `clear`, `grabbable`, `promote`, `breaks`, `split`, `released`), or None. `ensure_bubble(point)` gives the bubble on the body under `point` (planet space), made there with fine and coarse `TerrainCollider`s (`fine`, `coarse`) when there is none on that body; `drop_bubble()` removes it with its trunks, rocks and player, takes its props out of the physics and calls `on_drop()` when set. `spawn(feet, heading)` puts a `character.Player` (`player`) with its feet at `feet` (planet space) in the bubble with its `gravity_gun.Gun` (`gun`, grabbing the props and rocks, of `gun_params`, a `GunParams` or None for its defaults, refusing those `too_hot(body)` says are too hot when it is set); `remove_player()`. Each frame: `read_controls(input)` fills `controls` and `gun_controls` from `engine.input` (W S A D, left Shift to run, left Ctrl to crouch, Space to jet; while the mouse is captured, the right button grabs and the left punts, and the mouse turns the view (by `mouse_sensitivity` times its movement, 1 at first), or with R held the held body; on a gamepad the left stick walks, the right looks (`PAD_LOOK_PIXELS`), A jumps and jets, the triggers grab and punt; with `invert_y` (False at first) the mouse's and the right stick's movement up and down turns the view the other way; `stick(value)` cuts out `GAMEPAD_DEAD_ZONE`) and keeps the view's turn in `look_delta`; `step(dt, viewpoint, camera, latency, active=True, before_step=None)` keeps the bubble around the player's feet, or without a player around `viewpoint` (dropping it once that is over another body or more than `Bubble.RADIUS` above the ground), rebases it, keeps its terrain meshes ahead of the focus by `latency` seconds, its trunks and rocks around `camera` within `COLLIDER_RADIUS`, its props in their states, and steps the physics by `dt` in steps of `STEP` (1/120 s, at most `MAX_STEPS` a frame): the player with `controls` while `active` (else standing still, and the gun drops what it holds), the gun, `before_step(bubble, fraction)`, the props' drag and the bubble; then strikes on rocks break or loosen them (from `strike_momentum`). `request_nearby(camera, bodies)` asks for the trees near the camera, and `end_frame()` follows the world's `end_frame`. `place_eye()` gives the eye in planet space and keeps the view's (right, up, forward) in `view`; `max_level(body, active=True)` is `FOOT_MAX_LEVEL` (14) for the body under an active player, else None; `head_bob` turns the player's head bob; `gm(body)`, `prop_state(prop)` ((body, position from its centre, velocity)) and `forget_body(body)` (its props removed, for a body built again).

`ground_patch.GroundPatch(radius, centre, spacing, sea=True, water=None)` is a grid of `SIZE` × `SIZE` (13 × 13) points `spacing` metres apart in the plane tangent to a sphere of `radius` at the unit direction `centre`, along `tangent_axes(centre)` (east, north); `directions` are the unit directions from the body's centre through them, row by row. `set_heights(heights)` takes the terrain's heights at those directions (with `sea`, below 0 counts as 0; with `water`, a function of a unit direction giving a water level or None, such as `PlanetWorld.lake_level`, below that level counts as it). `query(point)` takes a point relative to the body's centre and gives (depth, normal): how far it is below the surface along the surface's normal (negative above), with the height interpolated bilinearly in the grid's cell under it and the normal from that cell; points outside the grid take its nearest edge, and points more than half the grid's width outside it, or on the far side of the body, give None. `grid_coordinates(point)` is where the point's direction meets the plane, in grid steps from the first point.

Rocks: with `rocks` on, the scatter also places small rocks (on their own grid, within `ROCK_RANGE`) and boulders (on the trees' grid where no tree or bush grows, within the bushes' range) on the planet, by their biomes and slope, and on the moon, by slope. `rocks.TakenRocks(engine)` holds the keys of rocks the scatter leaves out: `add(key)` (False once `MAX_TAKEN` are held), `discard(key)`, `clear()`, `clear_body(index)`, `key in taken`, `len(taken)`; `upload()` sends its table to the `TakenRocks` buffer when it changed and gives its count. Where the ore richness is above `ORE_ROCKS` (0.3), the rocks are ore rocks: more of them (the small rocks' chance up by as much as 0.5, the boulders' by 0.15, reached at richness 0.6), each with its resource, its colour turned towards its resource's tint, with glowing specks in its resource's colour, and listed with its grade and resource. `is_rock(kind)` is True for the rock kinds and the scanned rocks (`SCAN_ROCKS`), and `rock_grid(kind)` is a rock kind's grid (1 for "rock", 0 for "boulder", 2 for a large scanned rock, 3 for a small one, `SMALL_SCAN_ROCKS`); `ROCK_COLOURS` (by body index), `rock_colour(body, grade=0.0, resource=FERRITE)` (an ore rock's turned towards its resource's `ORE_TINTS` by `ORE_TINT_SHARE` times 1 + its grade), and `tinted(colour, tint)` give a rock's colour as drawn; `DENSITY` (2,600 kg/m³) is a plain rock's density and `DENSITIES` / `density(resource)` a rock's by its resource id (ferrite 3,400, cuprite 3,000, quartz 2,650, amber 1,100, evaporite 2,200, ice 920, ilmenite 4,700, iridium 8,000 kg/m³; `DENSITY` for none); `SPECK_COLOURS` and `speck_colour(resource)` its specks' colour. `ORE_TINTS` and `SPECK_COLOURS` are `ore_colours.glsl`'s, by resource id. `vegetation.rock_points(name)` gives a rock kind's points for a convex hull and `rock_triangles(name)` its triangles (positions, normals, shades), one unit tall above its base and reaching `ROCK_SINK` below it.

## Biomes

Each of the planet's chunk vertices carries its climate (`climate.glsl`): a temperature that falls from 30 degrees C at the equator to -14 at the poles and by 8.5 degrees per kilometre of height, a moisture that is high at the equator and near 60 degrees of latitude, low near 30 degrees and at the poles and lower inland, and a cover noise about 400 m across; each with noise. The shaders (`biome.glsl`) add `temperature_offset` and `moisture_offset` and weigh eight biomes by temperature and moisture: desert, savanna, tropical forest, steppe, grassland, deciduous forest, taiga and tundra, blending at the edges between them. The biomes colour the ground and decide what grows: snow lies below -6 degrees (a slope facing the equator counting warmer, one facing the pole colder), sand on beaches, rock on steep slopes in strata, scree under cliffs (`geology.glsl`). `biomes.weights` and `biomes.dominant` classify as the shaders do; `biomes.card_rows(world.ground_climate)` names the biome under the viewpoint.

The probes also give the ore richness (`resources.glsl`), 0 to 1. On the planet it is ridged veins about 450 m across times patches about 1.7 km across, times 0.4 + 0.6 of the mountains' mask, and 0 below sea level: about 5 % of the land is above 0.3 and 1.4 % above 0.6. On the moon each of the craters of the three largest sizes has a hashed mineral value; one above 0.6 holds ore over its floor and rim, of grade rising from 0 at 0.6 to 1 at 1.

Where the richness is above 0, the probes and the ore rocks also have a resource (`resources`: `FERRITE`, `CUPRITE`, `QUARTZ`, `AMBER` and `EVAPORITE` on the planet, `ICE` and `ILMENITE` on the moon; `IRIDIUM` (8), which the shaders never choose, for meteorites; `NONE` is 0, and `name(id)` names one). On the planet each of the five has a weight from the base climate (without the climate settings' offsets) and the mountains' mask: ferrite in the mountains' bands (at most 0.55), cuprite in mild, dry ground, quartz in the cold, amber in warm, wet ground, evaporite in hot, dry ground. Each weight is multiplied by 1 + 0.6 times its own fBm about 11 km across, and the highest one wins (ferrite where they tie). With the default terrain about 42 % of the ore is ferrite, 20 % quartz, 17 % cuprite, 15 % amber and 6 % evaporite. `planet_weights(temperature, moisture, mountains)` and `planet_pick(weights, noise)` make the same choice in NumPy. On the moon an ore crater whose centre lies beyond `MOON_ICE_LATITUDE` (0.55, the sine of its latitude) holds ice, any other ilmenite (`moon_resource(crater_y)`).

`flora_hue` turns the plants' colours and the forests' and grass's on the ground around the colour wheel, `soil_hue` the bare ground's, keeping their brightness; `biomes.PALETTES` are named pairs of them (Earth, Crimson, Violet, Amber, Teal). The colour mode "biome" shows each biome in a flat colour.

## Vegetation

Trees (spruces, pines, two broadleaf trees, acacias; each species grown by `tree_generator` in `vegetation.VARIANTS` variants from the terrain's seed, `vegetation.forest(seed)`), bushes, shrubs and cacti grow on the planet within `vegetation_range` of the camera, placed on the GPU each frame by `VegetationScatter` by their biomes' chances and forms, coloured by their biomes, and drawn with sun and cloud shadows; the [planet example's README](../../../examples/python/simulations/planet/README.md) has how they are placed and drawn. They are built of bark and alpha-tested foliage cards showing an atlas that `setup` bakes (`vegetation_textures`): from the CC0 scans that `python -m shoonyakasha.planet.vegetation_textures` downloads into `SHOONYAKASHA_VEGETATION_TEXTURES` or `shoonyakasha/vegetation_textures` in the user's cache (`vegetation_scans` is True when they were used), else from leaves drawn in the module. Beyond `VEGETATION_NEAR` a tree is its impostor (`vegetation_impostors`), one quad facing the camera. The vegetation, the rocks and the grass are placed on the chunks of `sk.PlanetLod`'s scatter draws (`scatter_reach()`): the drawn chunks, every chunk within `SCATTER_NEAR` (60 m) of the camera, and every chunk from which a plant up to `TALLEST_PLANT` (22 m) high, or its shadow (away from the sun, as long as the tallest plant's at the camera, at most `SCATTER_SHADOW`, 100 m), reaches into view. Each kind of `vegetation.KINDS` but grass holds up to `VEGETATION_CAPACITY` plants in view at a time.

With `scans` (on by default) the scanned rocks and plants of `scans.SCANS` grow among them on the planet, once prepared: `python -m shoonyakasha.planet.scan_export` extracts the Poly Haven scenes' archives (`namaqualand.zip`, `pine_forest.zip`, `verdant_trail.zip`, from `SHOONYAKASHA_SPACE_ASSETS`, else `assets/space_assets` under the current directory) and runs Blender headless (`SHOONYAKASHA_BLENDER`, else the newest installed) with the scenes' own scripts turned off. Each chosen object is decimated to its triangle budget, its albedo (with its alpha) and normal map written at 1K, and a rock's convex hull of at most 64 points kept, into `SHOONYAKASHA_SCANS` or `shoonyakasha/space_assets` in the user's cache, with a `CREDITS.md`; `--check` reports each kind. `setup` packs their materials into an albedo and a normal atlas of 512-texel cells. The large scans (boulders, rock faces, quiver trees, shrubs, logs) lie on a grid of 19 m cells within `SCAN_RANGE` (1.2 km), the small ones (stones, flowers, ferns, dry branches) on one of 4.8 m within 250 m, kept by their family's chance where they stand: the desert's on dry ground, the forest floor's under the trees' cover, the rocks' on rock and dry ground. A scan is drawn with its own albedo and normal map, a cut-out material lit as foliage. The scanned rocks are rocks: listed by `NearbyTrees` with their keys on grid 2 (large) or 3 (small), and left out by the scatter where their cells are taken. Without the prepared scans none grow.

Outposts: `outpost_kit.build(name, rng, **params)` builds a part of `PARTS` (pad, habitat, dome, container_stack, tank, tank_farm, pipe, ore_line, mast, solar, antenna, barrier, terminal, ore_bin) in its own frame (x right, y up, z ahead, standing on y = 0): a `Part` with its footprint's half extents and its pieces, each a `Piece` with its solid triangles (vertex coloured; a lamp's fixture among them), its lamps' glow (in `LAMP`, `LAMP_OVER` larger than the fixture), its screens (in `SCREEN`), its hulls (at most 64 points each) and, for containers and barriers, `dynamic` with a mass. `Geometry` collects flat-shaded triangles facing out (`box`, `cylinder`, `dome`, `mound`, `add`; `arrays()` gives positions, normals and colours, float32 N x 3). `outpost_layout.purposes(seed, bodies, fixed)` gives each outpost one of `PURPOSES` (mining, fuel, research, trade), seeded, those on one body differing while there are purposes left, `fixed` naming outposts' purposes. `layout(seed, name, purpose, pad_size, pad_colour, equator, tint)` places an outpost's parts as `Placed` (name, centre x east and z north of the pad, heading from north towards east, params), the same for the same seed and name: the pad at the centre facing a seeded approach, the terminal and ore bin beside it, the purpose's parts (`CONTENTS`) between `INNER` and `OUTER` facing the centre with footprints `GAP` apart and off the approach corridor (`CORRIDOR` wide), solar arrays leaning towards `equator`, a mast near the edge and barriers in arcs beyond `OUTER`. `site_model(placed, body_radius, seed)` builds them into a `SiteModel` in the site's frame, each part lowered by the body's curvature: the static triangles, the loose pieces (`Loose`), the lamps' and screens' triangles, the bodies (`Body`: a hull's points, its frame's position and heading, dynamic or not, its mass), the beacons and the ore bins; `merged(loose)` puts the loose pieces' triangles together.

Tufts of grass grow within `grass_range` of the camera, placed by `GrassScatter` on a finer grid by their biomes' grass chances, and drawn by the same `Vegetation` pass as the last kind, crossed cards in their biomes' grass colour, swaying in the wind; they cast no shadows. Up to `GRASS_CAPACITY` tufts are in view at a time. `vegetation` and `vegetation_density` apply to the grass too.

## Cameras

Both have `update(dt, active=True)` once a frame (`active` False while the pointer is over the user interface), `scroll(steps)` for the mouse wheel, `position` and `basis` for `end_frame`, and `body`.

`GlobeCamera(world, body=None, target=None, range_=20000.0, heading=0.0, tilt=0.0)` looks at a target point on a body's ground (`target`, a direction from its centre) from `range` metres, turned `heading` radians from north and tilted `tilt` from straight down:

| Input | |
|---|---|
| Left button, drag | Grabs the ground: the view turns about the body's centre so the grabbed point stays under the pointer. Let go while moving and it coasts, slowing down |
| Wheel | Zooms by 20 % a step towards (or away from) the point under the pointer |
| Right or middle button, drag | Heading (sideways) and tilt (up and down, 0 to 85 degrees) |
| Double-click | Flies to the point under the pointer, 70 % closer |
| W, A, S, D; Q, E | Move the target over the ground at the range a second; turn the heading |

`look_at(body, target, range_, heading, tilt)` sets the view at once, `fly_to(target, range_, heading, tilt, seconds)` over time; `north_up()` and `straight_down()`. The target's height comes from the world's extra probe, which the camera asks for each frame; it keeps 2 m above the ground under it. The range runs from 5 m to 40 planet radii.

`FocusCamera(world, body=None, distance=None, yaw=0.0, pitch=20 degrees)` circles a body's centre at `distance` (3 radii by default), `yaw` around and `pitch` above the moon's orbital plane:

| Input | |
|---|---|
| Left button, drag | Circles the body; let go while moving and it coasts |
| Wheel | Zooms by 15 % a step, from 50 m above the ground to 40 planet radii |
| Click (pressed and let go within 5 pixels) | Focuses the body under the pointer: the view moves to it over 1.5 seconds, ending 3 of its radii from its centre |
| Q, E; R, F | Yaw; pitch |

`focus(body)` does the same as a click; `pick(cursor)` gives the body under a window point. `cursor_ray(cursor, size, basis, fov)` is the direction through a window point, `ray_sphere(origin, direction, centre, radius)` where a ray first meets a sphere.

## Settings

`sky_items(world, keys=None, site="the site")`, `cloud_items`, `ground_items` (with the vegetation's on, density and range, and the grass's on and range), `climate_items` (the temperature and moisture offsets, the flora's and soil's hues, and a button that sets the next palette, `next_palette(world)`) and `view_items(world, keys=None)` give items for `toolkit.overlay.SettingsMenu` that read and change the world's settings; `planet_items(world, edit, new_seed, keys=None)` gives a new seed (`new_seed()`, at once) and the terrain's parameters, held in `edit` (a `TerrainEdit(world)`) until its Apply builds the planet again (`TerrainEdit.apply`; `reset()` takes the planet's parameters again). `keys` maps an item's name to key names shown beside it.

## Ground textures

`python -m shoonyakasha.planet.ground_textures` downloads an albedo and a normal map at 1K for each of the terrain's layers (sand, dry ground, grass, forest floor, rock, snow and the moon's ground), CC0 scans from Poly Haven and ambientCG, with a `CREDITS.md`. They are kept in the `SHOONYAKASHA_GROUND_TEXTURES` directory, or else `shoonyakasha/ground_textures` in the user's cache (`%LOCALAPPDATA%` on Windows, `$XDG_CACHE_HOME` or `~/.cache` elsewhere). `PlanetWorld.setup` hands them to the pipeline with mipmaps when all are there, read with Pillow; without them the ground keeps its procedural colours.
